## Overview

The Difficulty & Phase Controller (DPC) is the run-arc authority for SLIPSTORM. Each tick during the RUNNING state, it polls the Run State Machine's `remaining_time` and emits two outputs: the **current run phase** (a discrete enum identifying which beat of the 60-second arc the run is in — slow opener, mid-escalation, or peak density) and a set of **difficulty parameters** that downstream systems consume to scale their own behavior (wave spawn cadence, telegraph window duration, and other scalars the run-shape demands). DPC owns no game entities and no visuals; it is a pure control layer whose only job is to map "where are we in the run?" to "what should the world be doing right now?" The player never sees DPC — they feel its shape. The opening seconds are deliberately undemanding: one wave at a time, generous telegraph, room to learn the rhythm. The middle squeezes — waves overlap, the telegraph tightens, the reaction window contracts. The final seconds are the peak: simultaneous pulls, the storm at its loudest. The run's emotional arc (Pillar 3) and the player's ability to feel themselves getting better at it (Pillar 5) both depend on this controller producing a curve steep enough to peak and shallow enough to remain readable. DPC is data-driven by design — phase boundaries and difficulty scalars are tuning knobs, not constants, so the curve can be re-tuned without code changes.

## Player Fantasy

A SLIPSTORM run is the storm learning to aim. Early seconds: it doesn't know you yet — debris arrives loose, magnetism slack, the world humming at a distance. Mid-run, it locks on — the pulling iron tightens around your line, waves overlap, telegraphs stack like layered breath, each onset still its own readable instant but pressed up against the next. By the final stretch, the storm has learned your shape — most of PEAK is the storm at its tightest: pulls come fast, **telegraphs at the floor and onsets butted edge-to-edge**, a rapid chain of readable beats where each new telegraph fires just as the prior one finishes. The reading task is no longer "track two telegraphs in flight" (MID) — it is "resolve one telegraph per beat at the floor cadence with no slack in between." And then, **once or twice per PEAK window — no more than three times, and never as the steady state — the storm commits**: two or three pulls land inside a single readable beat, a designed climax moment authored into the pattern pool as a *barrage*. The barrage is the *only* moment in the run when telegraph onsets cluster within a single readable window; the rest of PEAK delivers them edge-to-edge in sequence. The barrage is not how PEAK feels; it is what PEAK is *for*. The rest of the phase is the runway. Survival of a barrage is the moment the player carries out of the run.

The player never names this controller, and they never see it. They name the *shape*: "the part where it gets fast", "the bit before the end where everything hits at once", "the calm before". DPC has no UI, no telegraph of its own, no transition sting it owns. Its fantasy is delivered entirely through what downstream systems do *because* of it — wave density (Wave Spawner), telegraph window (Telegraph System), tempo (Audio Controller). The barrage moments themselves are a Wave Spawner concern: DPC binds the *that* (PEAK admits barrage patterns; OPENER and MID do not) and the *how much* (1–3 barrage events per PEAK on average, never the dominant pattern type); the *what* (barrage authoring, simultaneity tolerance, spatial layout) is Wave Spawner's domain (see Dependencies → Wave Spawner). Designers tuning the difficulty curve are tuning the storm's intelligence. If a tuning pass makes the mid-run feel passive, the PEAK feel arbitrary, or barrages become the steady state instead of the climax, the curve has failed this fantasy and must be re-shaped.

## Phase Identity

Each phase delivers a qualitatively distinct experience, not a scalar slide along a single "intensity" axis. This is what makes the three-phase model non-redundant: PEAK is not "MID but faster," and MID is not "OPENER but denser." Each phase makes a different *kind* of demand on the player.

| Phase | Phase identity (what the player feels here that they do not feel elsewhere) |
|---|---|
| **OPENER** | Reading the rhythm. Single waves arrive with the most generous telegraph the run will offer. The player calibrates the slip timing against a known-good window. |
| **MID** | Reading the *stack*. Telegraphs begin to overlap in flight; the player learns to track two telegraphs at once with a tightened-but-still-comfortable per-onset window. The hazard is parallel reads, not simultaneous arrivals. |
| **PEAK** | Reading the *commit*. The default state is telegraphs at the floor with onsets butted edge-to-edge: each new telegraph fires as the prior one ends (because the intra-pattern and cross-pattern stagger floor equals `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S`, which at PEAK equals `telegraph_window_s`, producing sequential rather than overlapping in-flight telegraphs). The reading task is "resolve one telegraph per beat at floor cadence with no slack between beats" — a different demand than MID's parallel-tracking. **PEAK's exclusive identity moment is the barrage**: 1–3 times per run, two or three pulls land inside a single readable window (≤ `BARRAGE_SIMULTANEITY_WINDOW_S` between onsets). The barrage is the climax beat the rest of the phase builds toward and recovers from. |

**Why PEAK is a distinct phase, not "MID accelerated":**

PEAK is justified as a separate phase by two structural differences from MID, only one of which is the density scalar:

1. **Telegraph cadence transitions from overlap-tracked to edge-to-edge sequential.** In MID, `telegraph_window_s = 0.82s` (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — Path B reauthoring per R6 Q7 binding rule under R10d FLOOR=0.70s; was 0.72s under pre-R10d FLOOR=0.6s) and the intra-pattern stagger floor is `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S = 0.70s` (R10d binding 2026-06-17; was 0.6s pre-R10d) — a 0.12s in-flight overlap window per pair of staggered telegraphs, demanding parallel reading of two telegraphs at once. In PEAK, `telegraph_window_s = TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S = 0.70s` — the stagger floor equals the telegraph window, so onsets land edge-to-edge with zero overlap. The reading task changes from "track two telegraphs in flight simultaneously" (MID) to "resolve one telegraph per beat at floor cadence with no recovery slack between beats" (PEAK). These are qualitatively different perceptual demands, not the same demand at a faster rate.

2. **Barrage admissibility.** PEAK is the only phase in which the design contract permits simultaneous-arrival telegraph onsets within a single pattern. OPENER and MID forbid it (intra-pattern stagger ≥ `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S` applies without exception). PEAK admits it under a bounded carve-out.

Without barrages, PEAK is still distinct from MID via the cadence-character shift in (1). With barrages, PEAK additionally delivers its exclusive climax-beat moments. Both differentiators are independent of the floor numeric value (see next paragraph).

**This identity holds at any floor numeric value PROVIDED `TelegraphWindowCurve` is re-authored under Path B.** (R6 correction — game-designer Q7 BLOCKING; **R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — the R10d FLOOR raise 0.65 → 0.70s mechanically triggered Path B per this rule; pre-R10d curve keys have been replaced by `(0.0, 0.94), (0.333, 0.82), (0.75, 0.70), (1.0, 0.70)` per the general rule below**.) The prior version of this paragraph claimed structural independence without acknowledging that `telegraph_window_s = max(TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S, TelegraphWindowCurve.GetFloatValue(t_norm))` clamps **all three phases** when the floor rises. Concrete walk under R10d (was R6 hypothetical at 0.9s; replaced with the actual R10d 0.70s case for traceability): under R10d FLOOR=0.70s, the pre-R10d `TelegraphWindowCurve` keys `(0.0, 0.9), (0.333, 0.72), (0.75, 0.6), (1.0, 0.6)` would clamp post-floor to `(0.9, 0.72, 0.70, 0.70)` — MID's `telegraph_window_s` (curve key 0.72) sits only 0.02s above the new floor (was 0.12s above the pre-R10d floor 0.6s), collapsing MID's design-bound 0.12s in-flight overlap window by 83% to 0.02s. Differentiator 1 (cadence-character shift MID→PEAK) is silently destroyed without Path B reauthoring. The R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 reauthoring of the curve keys (see Tuning Knobs `TelegraphWindowCurve` row) restores the 0.12s intra-phase overlap window under the new FLOOR.

**Path B reauthoring requirement (binding — R7 game-designer BLOCKING fix, closes Seam 2 OPENER inversion; R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — Path B mechanically TRIGGERED by R10d FLOOR raise; current production keys reflect the post-Path-B values, NOT the pre-R10d defaults)**: If AC-PILLAR-2-CONCURRENT Path B fires OR if FLOOR rises by any other mechanism (e.g., R10d CD ruling), `TelegraphWindowCurve` MUST be re-authored to preserve **both** the OPENER→MID telegraph contraction AND MID's overlap window. The binding rules:

- **MID's key at `t_norm = 0.333` MUST equal `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S + intra_phase_overlap_s`** where `intra_phase_overlap_s` is bound at `0.12s` (matches the design-bound MID overlap at the original 0.6s floor). At R10d floor=0.70s (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 current binding): MID key becomes `(0.333, 0.82s)`. At hypothetical Path B floor=0.9s: MID key becomes `(0.333, 1.02s)`.
- **OPENER's key at `t_norm = 0.0` MUST equal `MID's key + intra_phase_overlap_s`** (= `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S + 2 × intra_phase_overlap_s`). At R10d floor=0.70s: OPENER key becomes `(0.0, 0.94s)`. At hypothetical Path B floor=0.9s: OPENER key becomes `(0.0, 1.14s)`. This preserves OPENER's identity as the run's *most generous* telegraph phase (Phase Identity table line above: "OPENER = the most generous telegraph the run will offer") AND maintains a 0.12s phase-to-phase telegraph contraction across OPENER→MID→PEAK. Without this OPENER bump, MID's reauthored key would exceed OPENER's pre-reauthor key, inverting Phase Identity and making MID the most relaxed phase — a Pillar 3 violation (the run-arc inverts).
- **PEAK's key at `t_norm = 0.75` MUST equal `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S`** by design intent (the cadence-character shift requires PEAK at the floor) — automatic at any floor value since the curve key floors at the runtime floor.

**Production keys under R10d FLOOR=0.70s (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — current binding)**: `(0.0, 0.94), (0.333, 0.82), (0.75, 0.70), (1.0, 0.70)`. Telegraph contraction is `0.94s → 0.82s → 0.70s` — 0.12s per phase transition. Differentiator 1 (cadence shift MID→PEAK from overlap-tracked to edge-to-edge sequential) is preserved because MID retains 0.12s overlap relative to PEAK at the new floor. Differentiator 2 (barrage admissibility) is unaffected by floor changes. **Authored example under hypothetical further Path B floor=0.9s**: keys `(0.0, 1.14), (0.333, 1.02), (0.75, 0.9), (1.0, 0.9)`.

**General rule** (for any floor value `f`): OPENER key ≥ `f + 2 × intra_phase_overlap_s`, MID key ≥ `f + intra_phase_overlap_s`, PEAK key ≥ `f`. The original pre-R10d example keys (`(0.0, 0.9), (0.333, 0.72), (0.75, 0.6), (1.0, 0.6)`) satisfied these rules at floor f=0.6s with equality (production default pre-R10d); the R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 keys above are the current production default at R10d FLOOR=0.70s, and the Path B keys at f=0.9s are the binding example for any further raise.

With this reauthoring, Differentiator 1 (cadence shift) and Differentiator 2 (barrage admissibility — OPENER and MID still cannot use `is_barrage=true` patterns) both survive any floor value. Path B is therefore admissible IF the designer accepts the implied reauthoring burden. Without the reauthoring, Path B silently collapses PEAK and MID into a single cadence regime. The Path B resolution path in Rule 9 inherits this reauthoring requirement.

**Barrage cadence**: 1–3 barrage events per PEAK window, target ~2 on average. Lower bound 1 ensures every PEAK has at least one climax beat (without it the phase identity is unobservable in a given run). Upper bound 3 keeps barrage as a moment, not a steady state (above 3 in a 15-second PEAK at default `RUN_DURATION_S` the player parses every pattern as a barrage and the contrast — the thing that makes barrage feel like a commit — collapses). The cadence is bound here at the identity layer; the Wave Spawner GDD implements it via the static pool composition (see Dependencies).

**Pillar attribution of phase identity** (mirrors the R3 sting reattribution — NOT a Pillar 5 mechanism): PEAK identity is delivered primarily through **Pillar 3** (the 60-second arc reaches its climax in the barrage beats) and **Pillar 4** (the world's behavior, not UI, communicates the escalation peak). **Pillar 5** is the survivability constraint on barrage authoring (see Pillar Alignment) — not the delivery vehicle for PEAK identity.

## Pillar Alignment Under Option B (Barrage Carve-Out)

Option B preserves the simultaneous-pulls fantasy at the climax moment while bounding the readability risk. The pillar consequences are:

**Pillar 1 — Slip Is the Only Verb**: **Unaffected.** Barrages are denser slip moments, not new verbs. A barrage of three concurrent pulls is still resolved by lateral slip; no new mechanic is required. If a barrage were ever to require a verb other than slip (e.g., a dash, a parry), it would violate Pillar 1 and must be redesigned as a pattern Wave Spawner authoring rejects.

**Pillar 2 — The World Telegraphs Before It Strikes**: **Strengthened on the default case, bounded on the carve-out case.** The intra-pattern stagger constraint still applies as the default rule for all OPENER and MID patterns and for non-barrage PEAK patterns. At PEAK in the non-barrage default, the stagger floor equals the telegraph window, producing **edge-to-edge sequential onsets** rather than overlapping ones — each telegraph is fully readable before the next begins, the tightest sustainable single-onset reading task the design permits. The cross-pattern stagger gap R4 surfaced is closed by the cross-pattern stagger policy (see Wave Spawner contract). The carve-out is narrow and typed: only patterns flagged `is_barrage=true` in the PEAK pool may author within-pattern simultaneous onsets, and only up to **M=3 pulls** within **W ≤ 0.3s** of each other. Barrage frequency is capped at **1–3 events per PEAK window**, average ~2. Risk-bound mechanism: barrages are *designed events* in static pool authoring, not emergent overlaps from runtime composition. The risk surface is `barrage_pattern_count * average_barrage_exposures_per_run`, which is bounded by the PEAK pool authoring rules (see Wave Spawner contract). A separate playtest-gated AC — **AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE** — validates barrage survivability at Alpha (parallel to AC-PILLAR-2-CONCURRENT for non-barrage edge-to-edge sequential load). Without AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE, the carve-out is unvalidated at Alpha.

**Pillar 3 — Sixty Seconds Is the Whole Game**: **Strengthened.** The barrage event is the run's climax beat — the moment the 60-second arc was building toward. Pillar 3 requires that "a complete emotional arc — calm, escalation, peak, resolution — fits inside one run." Without barrages, PEAK delivers escalation but not a discrete *peak moment* the player can name. The 1–3 barrage events per PEAK are the peak in "PEAK." This also retains the R3 CD-bound attribution of the phase-transition sting under Pillar 3 (run-arc legibility, primary): the sting at MID→PEAK announces "the arc has entered its climax phase," which the barrage then realizes.

**Pillar 4 — Surreal in Motion, Not in Menus**: **Strengthened (secondary on sting; unchanged on UI prohibition).** The PEAK-onset sting attribution as Pillar 4 secondary (R3 CD-bound, R4 HOLD THE LINE) is unchanged. Option B deepens the Pillar 4 surface: the barrage *is* the world's tonal escalation made literal — the storm commits, the music does not announce it, no HUD phase indicator appears. The barrage is felt through wave behavior and audio mix, never through chrome. The Pillar 4 forbidden-HUD list extends implicitly: no "barrage incoming" warning, no special UI indicator on barrage patterns. The barrage's only "telegraph" is the same telegraph every pull uses — clustered.

**Pillar 5 — Skill Is Visible**: **Unaffected by barrage carve-out.** (R6 correction — game-designer Q2 BLOCKING / qa-lead implicit.) The prior version of this section attributed barrage survivability to Pillar 5; that attribution was inconsistent with the AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE methodology (perceptual readability test on first-encounter, structurally identical to AC-PILLAR-2-CONCURRENT). The R3 reversal logic — "a timer-driven event cannot serve skill-visibility because Pillar 5 is about death-attribution / no-mystery-RNG" — applies cleanly here too: barrage *readability* (can the player perceive what's incoming?) is a Pillar 2 concern, not a Pillar 5 one. Pillar 5 is unaffected by the barrage carve-out. The misattribution is hereby corrected; see Pillar 2 above for the load-bearing analysis.

**Pillar 2 update (barrage survivability binding)**: **Constraint imposed by barrage carve-out**: every barrage configuration must be a survivable triage. Wave Spawner asset-authoring tooling SHOULD validate this at content-cook time using the readability envelope defined below (spatial separation between concurrent telegraph onsets; slip distance per `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S` once Pull-Wave GDD supplies the derived constant). Playtest-gated validation: **AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE** with sample size n ≥ 30, success threshold ≥ 80% barrage-event survival on first-encounter, parallel to AC-PILLAR-2-CONCURRENT methodology. The carve-out's Pillar 2 envelope is bound by the runtime constants M=3 and W≤0.3s plus the spatial constant introduced under Wave Spawner Contract Definitions.

**Summary**: Option B preserves the simultaneous-pulls fantasy at exactly the moments the design wanted it (climax beats), bounds the Pillar 2 risk via typed carve-out + Wave Spawner asset validation + a new playtest AC. No pillar is weakened. No R3 CD adjudication (sting attribution) is reopened. Pillar 5 is **unaffected** by the carve-out (R6 reattribution); the barrage's readability is Pillar 2's concern.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **Single authority**. The Difficulty & Phase Controller (DPC) is the exclusive authority for the run-phase and difficulty-parameter values during a RUNNING run. No other system may compute or override these values.

2. **Poll, don't subscribe**. DPC reads `remaining_time` from the Run State Machine each tick. DPC does not subscribe to `OnStateChanged` or `OnPausedChanged`.

3. **Tick prerequisite — ForceTickNow pull semantics (uniform across all OQ-7 paths, R6 fix)**. DPC must execute its tick *after* RSM has updated `remaining_time` for the current frame and *before* any downstream consumer reads DPC's outputs. **DPC's own UE type (GDD-bound)**: `class UDPCController : public UObject, public FTickableGameObject` (multiple inheritance, UObject declared first per UE5 convention so the GENERATED_BODY() reflection layout takes precedence; FTickableGameObject is the mixin). The `FTickableGameObject` mixin self-registers on construction, so DPC is ticked automatically by the engine's tickable-object pass. Required `FTickableGameObject` overrides: `Tick(float DeltaTime)` (DeltaTime parameter mandatory by the contract; used by EC-13's watchdog accumulator during the async-load window — see EC-13; NOT used for `t_norm` computation, which is derived from RSM's `remaining_time` per Rule 5 — see R-3 correction R7); **`IsTickable()` returns `true` unconditionally** (R7 BLOCKING fix — Path A resolution, closes unreal-specialist B-1 internal contradiction); the engine ticks DPC at all times, including during the async-load window. The `Tick()` body internally gates on `bInitialized` (Rule 14) for **active gameplay publication** but **always** advances `watchdog_elapsed_s` and **always** publishes the pre-init inactive snapshot when `bInitialized == false`. This preserves EC-13's Bound-and-Abort mechanism and aligns with the lines 349/556 contract that "every DPC tick during the load window publishes the pre-init inactive snapshot." `GetStatId()` returns `GET_STATID(STAT_DPCTick)`; `IsTickableInEditor()` returns `false`; `IsTickableWhenPaused()` returns `false` (Rule 4's gate handles pause; engine-level paused-tick is not needed). Test construction pattern (Seam 7): `TStrongObjectPtr<UDPCController> DPC(NewObject<UDPCController>(...));` is the R6-recommended idiom (RAII-safe, automatic GC root + automatic release on scope exit, no leak on test assertion firing mid-test). Legacy `NewObject + AddToRoot + explicit RemoveFromRoot` pattern remains documented in Seam 7 with the leak-on-assertion caveat. **Production construction outer**: in production, DPC is owned by a runtime-context outer specified by the OQ-7 ADR (e.g., `UGameInstance` or the RSM subsystem's wrapper); the production outer keeps DPC alive without explicit rooting. The OQ-7 ADR MUST specify DPC's production outer to fully resolve the lifetime contract.

   **RSM → DPC tick-ordering mechanism — ForceTickNow pull semantics, uniform across all three OQ-7 RSM-type outcomes (R6 unreal-specialist B-1 fix)**: The prior R5 enforceability matrix referenced `FTickFunction::AddPrerequisite()` as a "hard contract" path for `AActor`/`UActorComponent` RSM types. That mechanism is factually unavailable: `FTickableGameObject` (DPC's chosen type) does NOT have a `FTickFunction` member, so `FTickFunction::AddPrerequisite()` cannot be called *from* DPC's side onto RSM's tick function in any OQ-7 outcome. The matrix is hereby collapsed: **on ALL three OQ-7 paths (RSM = AActor / UActorComponent / UWorldSubsystem / UTickableWorldSubsystem / UGameInstanceSubsystem), Rule 3 is enforced via pull semantics — DPC calls `RSM->ForceTickNow()` as the first statement of its own tick body (after `SCOPE_CYCLE_COUNTER` and `check(IsInGameThread())`).** This guarantees RSM's `remaining_time` is current before DPC reads it, regardless of UE scheduler ordering or RSM's chosen type. Rule 3 is a hard contract on all paths under this single mechanism. EC-4's runtime canary remains as a defense-in-depth detection mechanism but is no longer the sole detection on any path. The OQ-7 ADR is unchanged in scope; the ordering mechanism for RSM → DPC is no longer ADR-dependent.

   **Forward-imposed contract on RSM GDD (expanded R6 — 5 items, was 1)**: `IRSMTimeStateProvider` (or its concrete UE implementation) must expose:
     - **(1) `ForceTickNow()` primitive — signature `void ForceTickNow()`, no parameters.** Internally invokes RSM's tick body with `DeltaTime = FApp::GetDeltaTime()` IF RSM has not yet ticked this frame (tracked via an RSM-internal `bHasTickedThisFrame` flag). If RSM has already ticked, `ForceTickNow()` is a no-op. Idempotent within a single engine frame. Game-thread only.
     - **(2) `bHasTickedThisFrame` flag must be set by BOTH the regular Tick path AND the ForceTickNow path (R6 unreal-specialist B-3 fix)**: RSM's regular `Tick(float DeltaTime)` MUST set `bHasTickedThisFrame = true` as its first statement (after thread checks). Without this, the engine could tick RSM first (registration order), regular Tick advances `remaining_time`, then DPC's ForceTickNow sees flag==false and re-runs RSM's tick body — double-advancing `remaining_time`. The flag is reset at frame boundary via `FCoreDelegates::OnEndFrame`. **OnEndFrame delegate handle lifetime (R6 unreal-specialist R-4)**: RSM stores the `FDelegateHandle` returned by `FCoreDelegates::OnEndFrame.AddRaw(this, ...)` (or `.AddUObject(this, ...)` if RSM is UObject-derived) as a member and explicitly removes the binding (`FCoreDelegates::OnEndFrame.Remove(Handle)`) in RSM's `Deinitialize()` or destructor. Without explicit removal, the delegate fires against a destroyed RSM on PIE shutdown or level transition.
     - **(3) Non-callback invariant (binding)**: `ForceTickNow()` MUST NOT invoke any subscriber callback, delegate, observer notification, or downstream consumer call during its execution. The primitive only updates RSM's internal state (`remaining_time`, `is_paused`, `current_state` if applicable). Subscribers receive their updates on RSM's next scheduled tick or via their own polls. **State-transition visibility note (R6 unreal-specialist B-3 expansion)**: if `remaining_time` reaches 0 inside ForceTickNow, RSM transitions RUNNING→COMPLETE. The transition is observable to DPC immediately on the same call (DPC reads `current_state` after `ForceTickNow()` returns). Non-DPC subscribers (Wave Spawner, Audio Controller, UI) that use `OnStateChanged` callbacks observe the transition only on RSM's next regular tick — a 1-frame latency. Subscribers that poll see the transition the next time they poll. RSM GDD must explicitly accept this 1-frame latency as a documented invariant for callback-based subscribers. Wave Spawner and Audio Controller may rely on polls (snapshot reads) rather than callbacks to eliminate the latency.
     - **(4) External abort entry (binding, R6 unreal-specialist B-4 / new forward contract from EC-13)**: RSM must accept an external `RequestAbort(reason: EDPCAbortReason)` call mid-RUNNING and transition to ABORTED. Currently RSM lists ABORTED reachable only from `PAUSED_TIMEOUT_S`-exceeded on foreground; the external-abort path is new and must be added to RSM's state machine. The `IDPCAbortDelegate` interface (DPC-defined, RSM-implemented in production) is the binding contract.
     - **(5) Thread**: All contract surface (ForceTickNow, RequestAbort) executes on the game thread.
     - **RSM GDD revision is the NEXT BLOCKING ITEM after DPC R6 approves**: these 5 items must land in RSM GDD before DPC implementation can proceed.

4. **Active only in RUNNING**. DPC's outputs are valid only when RSM `current_state == RUNNING` and `is_paused == false`. In all other states and during `is_paused == true`, DPC publishes its **inactive default snapshot** (defined under States and Transitions) and does not evaluate the curves.

5. **Normalized progress**. DPC's internal timeline is the normalized progress value `t_norm`:
   ```
   t_norm = clamp((RUN_DURATION_S − remaining_time) / RUN_DURATION_S, 0.0, 1.0)
   ```
   `t_norm` is `0.0` at run start and `1.0` at run end. All curve evaluations and phase boundary tests use `t_norm`, never `remaining_time` directly. This makes the run arc invariant under changes to `RUN_DURATION_S` (RSM allows it to be tuned in `[10, 300]s`).

6. **Three phases**. DPC owns the enum, declared `UENUM(BlueprintType) enum class ERunPhase : uint8 { OPENER = 0, MID = 1, PEAK = 2 }`. The `uint8` underlying type is required for `UPROPERTY`-reflected fields (see Rule 10); the `BlueprintType` decoration allows Wave Spawner / Audio Controller Blueprint consumers to switch on phase. Phase is a pure function of `t_norm`:
   - `OPENER`: `t_norm ∈ [0.0, OPENER_END_NORMALIZED)`
   - `MID`: `t_norm ∈ [OPENER_END_NORMALIZED, PEAK_START_NORMALIZED)`
   - `PEAK`: `t_norm ∈ [PEAK_START_NORMALIZED, 1.0]`

   Defaults: `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED = 0.333` (≈ 20s elapsed at default 60s run), `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED = 0.75` (≈ 45s elapsed / 15s remaining at default 60s).

7. **Phase is step-valued**. `current_phase` snaps to its new value at the first tick where `t_norm` crosses the boundary. There is no enum interpolation. This is deliberate (Pillar 3 — run-arc legibility): downstream systems (Audio Controller, Wave Spawner) detect a phase change by comparing `current_phase` to its prior-frame value, allowing transition stings and pattern-pool swaps to fire at a deterministic moment. **Pillar attribution note**: an earlier version of this rule (and the EC-9 / Audio Controller / Visual/Audio Requirements sections) attributed the phase-transition sting to Pillar 5 ("Skill Is Visible"). That attribution was incorrect and was reversed by the creative-director at re-review 3 (2026-06-04). Pillar 5 is fundamentally about death-attribution / no-mystery-RNG — a timer-driven sting that fires at `t_norm = 0.333` regardless of player action announces a world-state change, not a skill signal. The sting's correct primary attribution is **Pillar 3** (the 60-second arc made audible) with **Pillar 4** (the world's tonal escalation through audio rather than UI) as secondary. The Player Fantasy "the storm learning to aim" is delivered tonally (Pillar 4) and on the arc cadence (Pillar 3), not via Pillar 5.

8. **Difficulty scalars are continuous**. The three numeric outputs are evaluated each tick from per-parameter `UCurveFloat` assets sampled at `t_norm`, with **output-side finite guards** AND safety floors applied at evaluation. The `t_norm` input is already guaranteed finite by Rule 14's input-side guard; the output-side guard catches malformed curve assets (NaN keys, designer-authored `+Inf` values, corrupted asset binary):
   ```cpp
   const float t_norm_f = static_cast<float>(t_norm);  // narrowing safe in [0,1]; see Rule 10

   const float wsi_raw = WaveSpawnIntervalCurve->GetFloatValue(t_norm_f);
   const float tw_raw  = TelegraphWindowCurve->GetFloatValue(t_norm_f);
   const float mcw_raw = MaxConcurrentWavesCurve->GetFloatValue(t_norm_f);

   if (!FMath::IsFinite(wsi_raw) || !FMath::IsFinite(tw_raw) || !FMath::IsFinite(mcw_raw))
   {
       // Malformed curve at runtime — publish inactive snapshot, log once per affected curve.
       LogOnceErrorPerCurve(LogDPC, TEXT("Non-finite curve return: wsi=%f tw=%f mcw=%f"), wsi_raw, tw_raw, mcw_raw);
       ensureAlways(false);
       PublishInactiveSnapshot();
       return;
   }

   wave_spawn_interval_s = FMath::Max(WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S, wsi_raw);
   telegraph_window_s    = FMath::Max(TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S,    tw_raw);
   // R5 Cluster B Item 2 fix: clamp the FLOAT before FloorToInt32. Wrapping the int32
   // result in Clamp does NOT protect against FloorToInt32(1e12f) → implementation-defined
   // ARM behavior, because the UB occurs inside FloorToInt32 before the int clamp sees it.
   const float mcw_clamped_f = FMath::Clamp(mcw_raw, static_cast<float>(MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR), static_cast<float>(MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_CAP));
   max_concurrent_waves  = FMath::FloorToInt32(mcw_clamped_f);
   ```
   The three floors (`WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S = 0.25s`, `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S = 0.70s` *R10d-bound; see Rule 9 for two-phase gate framing*, `MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR = 1`) are runtime invariants — they live in DPC code, not in curve configuration, so no designer authoring error can produce a degenerate run (continuous spawn from a zero interval, sub-floor telegraph, or zero concurrent waves). Continuous evaluation prevents perceptible "pop" between phases. `max_concurrent_waves` is `int32` and steps as the curve crosses integer boundaries; this is acceptable because Wave Spawner reads it as a discrete cap. **The output-side finite guard is required**: without it, `FMath::Max(0.25f, NaN)` returns NaN (NaN comparisons evaluate false in `<` / `>`), and `FMath::FloorToInt32(NaN)` is undefined behavior in C++ (the static_cast from floating-point NaN to integer is implementation-defined; on ARM targets commonly produces `INT_MIN`).

10. **Atomic snapshot publication**. At the end of each DPC tick, DPC writes an immutable per-frame snapshot struct. The struct is declared `USTRUCT(BlueprintType)` with `GENERATED_BODY()` so Blueprint-implemented consumers (Wave Spawner, Audio Controller) can read `GetCurrentFrameState()` as a `BlueprintCallable` return value, and so Death Replay can serialize captured snapshots through Unreal reflection. **Post-tick publication delegate (`OnPostTickFrameStatePublished`)** *(2026-06-23 /propagate-design-change Wave Spawner R3a closure — FC-3; forward contract from Wave Spawner R2a-2 tick-ordering pin)*: in addition to the polling-based `GetCurrentFrameState()` / `GetPreviousFrameState()` accessors, DPC broadcasts a non-dynamic multicast delegate `DECLARE_MULTICAST_DELEGATE_OneParam(FOnPostTickFrameStatePublished, const FDPCFrameState& /* PublishedState */)` at the END of each DPC tick — strictly AFTER the snapshot fields are overwritten (previous-frame ← old current; current ← new value), so subscribers reading the delegate's `PublishedState` parameter or calling `GetCurrentFrameState()` from inside the callback observe a consistent post-tick state. The delegate fires unconditionally each tick (active and inactive snapshots both publish; pre-init inactive snapshot during the async-load window per EC-13 also publishes). Wave Spawner's `UWaveSpawnerSubsystem::Initialize()` binds to this delegate via `OnPostTickFrameStatePublished.AddUObject(this, &UWaveSpawnerSubsystem::HandlePostTickFrameStatePublished)` and runs its admission decision inside that callback (Wave Spawner R2a-2) — this makes tick ordering structural (`RSM → DPC → WaveSpawner`) rather than tick-group-dependent. Rationale for non-dynamic over dynamic: Wave Spawner binds via lambda-friendly `AddUObject`; no Blueprint subscribers exist for this delegate (Blueprint-implemented consumers continue to use `GetCurrentFrameState()` polling per Rule 10's existing contract). **Re-entrancy**: subscribers MUST NOT call any DPC mutator from inside the callback (DPC has no public mutators by design — `ForceTickNow` is RSM-only per Rule 4 — so this is structurally precluded, but documented for downstream-author clarity). **Test stub**: Seam 6 `IDPCFrameStateProvider` test fixture exposes a public `BroadcastOnPostTickFrameStatePublished(const FDPCFrameState&)` method so tests can synthesize delegate-fire timing without driving a full DPC tick (Wave Spawner unit tests bind to a fake DPC stub and call this method directly).

    ```cpp
    USTRUCT(BlueprintType)
    struct FDPCFrameState
    {
        GENERATED_BODY()

        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        ERunPhase current_phase = ERunPhase::OPENER;

        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        float wave_spawn_interval_s = 4.0f;

        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        float telegraph_window_s = 0.94f;

        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        int32 max_concurrent_waves = 1;

        // diagnostic; not a decision input
        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        double t_norm = 0.0;

        // false during pause / non-RUNNING; consumers MUST discard phase-diff signals when
        // either side of the diff has is_active == false (see Rule 16, EC-2, EC-9, EC-10)
        UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly, Category = "DPC")
        bool is_active = false;
    };
    ```

    Every field is `UPROPERTY(BlueprintReadOnly)`. Without UPROPERTY tags, `USTRUCT(BlueprintType)` is decorative — Blueprint graphs cannot access individual fields from a `GetCurrentFrameState()` return, and Death Replay's Unreal-reflection-based serialization claim breaks. `ERunPhase` is `UENUM(BlueprintType) : uint8` per Rule 6, which is the prerequisite for being a UPROPERTY-able field type. Default-initializer values match the inactive snapshot (defined under States and Transitions), so a default-constructed `FDPCFrameState` is a valid inactive snapshot — important because USTRUCTs are default-constructed by the reflection system during reset/teardown.
    Downstream consumers read the snapshot **once per decision** (typically once per their own tick), never polling individual fields. This prevents mid-tick fragmentation where a consumer reads `current_phase` and `telegraph_window_s` on different lines spanning a phase boundary tick.

    Curve asset references on the owning DPC type are `TObjectPtr<UCurveFloat>` (UE 5.0+ mandatory for UPROPERTY-tracked UObject pointers — registers with GC, produces deterministic null on collection rather than a dangling pointer). Each `TObjectPtr<UCurveFloat>` field is `UPROPERTY()`-decorated on the DPC owning UObject type so GC tracking is active. The custom log category `LogDPC` is declared with `DECLARE_LOG_CATEGORY_EXTERN(LogDPC, Log, All)` in the DPC module's public header (`DPCLog.h`) and defined with `DEFINE_LOG_CATEGORY(LogDPC)` in the corresponding `.cpp`. The DPC stats group and cycle stat are declared in `DPCStats.h` with `DECLARE_STATS_GROUP(TEXT("DPC"), STATGROUP_DPC, STATCAT_Advanced)` + `DECLARE_CYCLE_STAT(TEXT("DPC Tick"), STAT_DPCTick, STATGROUP_DPC)`, and defined in `DPCStats.cpp` with `DEFINE_STAT(STAT_DPCTick)`; `DPCController.cpp` opens `Tick()` with `SCOPE_CYCLE_COUNTER(STAT_DPCTick)` as the first statement so the cycle counter scopes the entire tick body (AC-22b enforces this via source-grep).

11. **No state retention across phase boundaries**. DPC holds no per-phase entry/exit hooks, timers, or counters. Each tick is a pure function of `(t_norm, curve assets, knobs)`. The only inter-tick state is the previous-frame snapshot, retained solely so consumers can diff against `current_phase` without each maintaining its own previous value — exposed via `GetPreviousFrameState()`. **First-call contract (scoped — construction and hot-reload ONLY)**: `GetPreviousFrameState()` is initialized to return the current frame's snapshot before any tick has executed and immediately after a hot-reload that reconstructs DPC's UObject. At construction (and post-reload), both the current-frame and previous-frame snapshot fields are set to the inactive default with `is_active == false`. On each tick after the active snapshot is computed, the previous-frame field is overwritten with the *old* current value before the current field is overwritten with the new value. This narrow contract prevents spurious transition firings ONLY in the construction-time and hot-reload paths. It does **NOT** protect against spurious diffs from pause/resume transitions (see Rule 16) or mid-run consumer subscription on boundary ticks (see Rule 17). EC-7 documents the testing of this scoped contract.

16. **Pause/resume diff suppression via `is_active`**. DPC publishes the inactive snapshot (with `is_active == false`) during pause and all non-RUNNING states (Rule 4); during `resume_grace` it publishes the active snapshot with `is_active == true` (EC-2). Consumers that detect phase transitions by snapshot diff (Wave Spawner, Audio Controller) **MUST** apply the following gate before firing any phase-transition behavior (sting, mix crossfade, pattern-pool swap):

    ```
    if (previous_snapshot.is_active == false || current_snapshot.is_active == false) {
        // Treat as inactive-boundary diff. Update consumer's local cache silently
        // (so the next active→active comparison is correct), but do NOT fire any
        // transition behavior. The pause-entry, pause-exit, and inactive-state
        // boundaries are not gameplay phase transitions and must not be audible.
        cached_phase = current_snapshot.current_phase;
        return;
    }
    if (previous_snapshot.current_phase != current_snapshot.current_phase) {
        // Real phase transition. Fire sting, swap pool, etc.
        ...
    }
    ```

    This gate is contractual — Audio Controller, Wave Spawner, Pull-Wave Behavior, and any future consumer that diffs phase MUST include it. Wave Spawner and Audio Controller GDDs will inherit this as a binding requirement.

    **Per-tick `is_active` rising-edge recovery (R6 BLOCKING fix — qa-lead BLOCKING 4)**: in addition to the diff-suppression gate above, every consumer MUST track its own last-observed `is_active` value (`prior_is_active`, initialized to `false`) and invoke its `is_active` rising-edge handler whenever the current tick's `is_active` transitions `false → true` on any tick after the first:
    ```
    if (prior_is_active == false && current_snapshot.is_active == true) {
        invoke_is_active_rising_edge_handler();
    }
    prior_is_active = current_snapshot.is_active;
    ```
    This contract sits on the consumer side (DPC publishes the snapshot; per-tick recovery detection is consumer state). The prior R5 version of Rules 16/17 covered first-tick rising edge (Rule 17) and inactive-state diff suppression (Rule 16) but did NOT specify per-tick rising-edge recovery on subsequent ticks — leaving an implementation gap where Audio Controller's mix-resume on pause-exit (EC-10) was only enforced via the test stub's behavior, not via a contractual rule. R6 closes this gap with the explicit per-tick recovery rule above. AC-RISING-EDGE's contrast sub-case (subscription during pause, then resume) is now verifying contractual behavior, not stub-internal behavior.

17. **Mid-run consumer subscription**. A consumer registering as a snapshot reader mid-run (e.g., a HUD widget that opens during PEAK, or a lazily-instantiated audio listener) must follow the **two-layer initialization protocol** below. The protocol distinguishes *phase-diff behavior* (firing transition handlers such as phase-transition stings, pool swaps, mix crossfades) from *`is_active` rising-edge behavior* (responding to `is_active` transitioning `false → true`, such as Audio Controller's mix-resume onset — see Dependencies → Audio Controller item 5). These two behaviors have different skip rules:

    **`is_active` rising-edge**: MUST be published and MUST be handled on the first tick after subscription if DPC is currently publishing an active snapshot (`is_active == true`). The first observed tick for the new consumer IS the rising edge of `is_active` from the consumer's perspective. Skipping the rising-edge response would silence the mix-resume signal that Audio Controller item 5 depends on. A consumer subscribing mid-RUNNING observes `is_active == true` on its first tick; that is the rising edge — it must respond to it.

    **Phase-diff behavior**: MUST be skipped on the consumer's first tick after subscription. Firing a phase-transition sting or pool swap based on the difference between `GetPreviousFrameState().current_phase` and `GetCurrentFrameState().current_phase` on the first tick is incorrect because `GetPreviousFrameState()` reflects the prior frame's DPC state, not the consumer's prior-observed state. A consumer subscribing mid-PEAK would see `MID → PEAK` in the diff if the phase boundary just occurred — but the consumer was not present for the MID phase and should not fire the PEAK sting retroactively.

    **Consumer-side initialization protocol** (two-layer):

    1. On first tick after subscription:
       - Read `GetCurrentFrameState()` into local `cached_phase` field.
       - Read `GetCurrentFrameState().is_active`. If `is_active == true`, **immediately invoke the `is_active` rising-edge handler** (e.g., Audio Controller's mix-resume logic). This is NOT skipped.
       - Set `bFirstReadComplete = false` (or equivalent sentinel) to suppress phase-diff handler for this tick.
    2. On all subsequent ticks:
       - Set `bFirstReadComplete = true` on the second tick entry.
       - Apply Rule 16's `is_active` gate before phase-diff handler.
       - Run phase-diff handler normally: compare `cached_phase` to `GetCurrentFrameState().current_phase`.

    **Why this differs from the prior version (R5 Cluster B Item 9 fix)**: the prior Rule 17 said "skip one tick" without distinguishing between `is_active` rising-edge and phase-diff behavior. This silenced the `is_active` rising-edge on mid-run subscription — a bug for Audio Controller item 5 (mix-resume). The corrected rule skips only the *phase-diff handler* and explicitly does NOT skip the `is_active` rising-edge response.

    **Rule 16 interaction**: Rule 16's `is_active` gate handles the case where subscription happens during pause or inactive state (`is_active == false` on the first tick → the consumer should NOT fire either `is_active` rising-edge or phase-diff handler; it simply caches the inactive snapshot and waits for the next active tick). Rule 17 handles subscription during active RUNNING (`is_active == true` on first tick → rising edge fires, phase-diff handler is skipped for one tick). These two rules cover all subscription timing scenarios.

    This contract sits on the consumer side because DPC has no per-consumer registration interface. AC-RISING-EDGE tests Rule 17's rising-edge guarantee during RUNNING; EC-7 tests the construction-path initialization only (NARROW SCOPE — see EC-7). This rule is forward-imposed on every consumer GDD via its Dependencies entry.

12. **No randomness, no internal events**. DPC does not consume an RNG. It does not emit `OnPhaseChanged` delegates or any events; the phase change is observable by snapshot diff. This keeps DPC deterministic — same `remaining_time` history always produces the same output sequence — which is required for replay correctness.

13. **Pause behavior is free**. Because RSM returns `cached_remaining_at_pause_entry` while `is_paused == true` (RSM EC-11), `t_norm` freezes during pause. Rule 4's gate publishes the inactive snapshot during pause; on `is_paused == false` resumption and `current_state == RUNNING`, DPC resumes curve evaluation from `t_norm` matching the resumed `remaining_time`.

14. **Curve and configuration preconditions** (validated at `Initialize()` for `UWorldSubsystem` / `UGameInstanceSubsystem`, or `BeginPlay()` for `AActor` / `UActorComponent` — actual lifecycle hook bound by the OQ-7 ADR's chosen UE object type. If curve assets are async-loaded via `FStreamableHandle`, validation defers until the handle reports complete):
    - **Curves**: All three `UCurveFloat` references must be non-null. Each curve must have at least one key at or before `t_norm = 0.0` and at least one key at or after `t_norm = 1.0` — this guarantees that `GetFloatValue()` interpolates rather than extrapolates across DPC's evaluation domain, so pre/post-infinity extrapolation modes are immaterial (startup validation logs a warning if the key range does not span `[0.0, 1.0]`). If any curve's *unclamped* evaluation at any sampled `t_norm` would dip below its corresponding floor, log a configuration warning at startup, surfacing tuning errors rather than silently flooring them.
    - **Threshold ordering and minimum MID width**: `check(OPENER_END_NORMALIZED + MIN_MID_WIDTH_NORMALIZED <= PEAK_START_NORMALIZED)` with `MIN_MID_WIDTH_NORMALIZED = 0.10` (constant; non-tunable). The 0.10 floor exists so MID always retains at least 10% of the run-arc — preventing a degenerate "binary OPENER-or-PEAK" config that satisfies strict `<` ordering with a sub-perceptible MID. `0.10 * RUN_DURATION_S` at minimum 10s is 1 second of MID; at 60s default is 6 seconds. Below 1 second of MID, phase transitions become a sub-perceptual flicker.
    - **Run duration**: `check(RUN_DURATION_S > 0.0)` read from RSM at DPC initialization. RSM also owns its own `[10, 300]s` range assertion (per RSM Tuning Knobs), but DPC adds a defensive `> 0` guard because F-1 divides by this value. If the assertion fails (release builds with asserts stripped), DPC publishes the inactive snapshot for the entire app lifetime and logs `LogDPC, Error` (with `ensureAlways(false)`) once at startup. The corresponding shipping-build guard (parallel to the `check`) is shown below.

    **Threshold ordering bounds (added separately because Rule 14's MID-width-minimum check does not catch boundary-of-domain violations):**
    - `check(OPENER_END_NORMALIZED > 0.0)`: rejects `OPENER_END = 0.0` (which would make OPENER permanently unreachable because F-2 evaluates `t_norm < OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` and F-1's `clamp` lower-bounds `t_norm` at `0.0`).
    - `check(PEAK_START_NORMALIZED < 1.0)`: rejects `PEAK_START = 1.0` (which would make PEAK observable for at most a single tick before RSM transitions to COMPLETE, defeating PEAK's role as the run's climax phase). Strict `<` because `= 1.0` produces a zero-effective-tick PEAK.
    - These two bounds are independent of the MID-width assertion: `OPENER_END + 0.10 <= PEAK_START` passes for `OPENER_END = 0.0, PEAK_START = 0.5` and for `OPENER_END = 0.5, PEAK_START = 1.5`, so the two boundary-of-domain bounds must be asserted separately.

    **Cap-vs-barrage compatibility check (R6 BLOCKING — Root Cause A, CD-bound; R7 mock-injection + check-ordering refinement)**: `check(MaxConcurrentWavesCurve.GetFloatValue(PEAK_START_NORMALIZED) >= static_cast<float>(MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE))` is required whenever the PEAK pattern pool contains at least one `is_barrage=true` pattern. **Mechanism (R7 refinement, closes systems-designer B-1 + qa-lead REC-3)**: this check runs **post-`OnCurvesLoaded`**, NOT at `Initialize()` — the check requires `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve` to be evaluable, which requires curves to be loaded. The `IWaveSpawnerPoolMetadataProvider` (Seam 10, see `docs/architecture/platform-seam-interfaces.md`) is injected at DPC construction alongside `IRSMTimeStateProvider*` and `IDPCAbortDelegate*` — DPC exposes `Inject(IRSMTimeStateProvider*, IDPCAbortDelegate*, IWaveSpawnerPoolMetadataProvider*)` (or equivalent constructor signature, mechanism delegated to the implementer; the GDD binds that all three injections complete before `OnCurvesLoaded` fires). **Pool-readiness contract**: by the time `OnCurvesLoaded` fires, Wave Spawner's pool composition MUST be stable — the production `FWaveSpawnerPoolMetadataProvider` either reads from a content-cooked manifest (synchronous, immediately available) or signals "not ready" by returning `false` and DPC treats `false` as a successful validation (no barrage carve-out concern). In tests, `FWaveSpawnerPoolMetadataTestStub` returns a test-fixture-configured boolean. **Mechanism summary**: at post-`OnCurvesLoaded`, DPC queries `IWaveSpawnerPoolMetadataProvider::HasBarrageInPeakPool()`; if `true`, DPC asserts the curve's PEAK key floors at ≥ `MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE`. Failure publishes the inactive snapshot for the run, logs `LogDPC, Error` once, and signals `IDPCAbortDelegate::RequestAbort(EDPCAbortReason::ThermalFallbackBarrageIncompatible)` so RSM transitions to ABORTED before the player observes the silent-deadlock state.

    **`bInitialized` semantics (R7 BLOCKING fix — closes systems-designer B-2 double-abort race, user Q2=A)**: `bInitialized` means **"validation concluded"**, NOT "validation succeeded." It is set to `true` at the end of `OnCurvesLoaded` regardless of validation outcome (success path: active publication enabled on subsequent ticks; failure path: inactive snapshot for run lifetime + abort already issued; either way validation is concluded, the watchdog has no further role). EC-13's watchdog cancellation gate `if (bInitialized) { watchdog_elapsed_s = 0.0; return; }` (line 563) fires correctly after validation failure, preventing the second `RequestAbort(AsyncLoadTimeout)` race at t=5s. The validation-outcome flag (active vs failed) is carried separately by `enum class EDPCInitOutcome : uint8 { Pending, Active, FailedAsyncLoadTimeout, FailedThresholdOrdering, FailedMinMidWidth, FailedOpenerZero, FailedPeakBeyondOne, FailedThermalFallbackBarrageIncompatible, FailedRunDurationZero, FailedNonFiniteRunDuration }` (or equivalent — names enumerated to match all Rule 14 failure paths). Active publication requires `eInitOutcome == Active`; all other outcomes hold the inactive snapshot. **Rationale**: at `max_concurrent_waves = 2` with `MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE = 3`, the barrage admission gate `available_slots ≥ required_slots` is mathematically unsatisfiable (available ≤ 2 < required = 3) — the barrage is deferred every spawn-decision cycle indefinitely, the drop rule re-seeds it into the shuffle-bag, the re-seeded barrage is redrawn and re-deferred — infinite loop that silently breaks the N≥1 structural guarantee and collapses Phase Identity on the thermal-fallback device. The runtime check converts this silent collapse into a loud failure. **Cook-time mirror**: Wave Spawner asset-authoring tooling MUST mirror this check at content cook time — any `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve` whose PEAK key evaluates below `MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE` (3) is a cook-time error when the PEAK pool contains barrage patterns. See Tuning Knobs section for the designer-facing thermal-fallback path under this constraint.

    `check()` failures fail-fast in development; in shipping builds (where `check` is a no-op by default), explicit parallel runtime guards run in addition to (not in place of) the `check()` macros — the GDD's `check()` mentions imply that the same condition is also guarded with `if (!cond) { UE_LOG(LogDPC, Error, ...); ensureAlways(false); /* publish inactive snapshot and return */ }` at the same call site. Reviewers must verify the parallel `if`/log path exists in code review; the `check()` alone does not satisfy the shipping-build guarantee. **Fatal-verbosity policy**: DPC never emits `UE_LOG(..., Fatal, ...)` for any condition described in this GDD. Fatal aborts the process unconditionally in all build configurations (via `FDebug::AssertFailed`), which (a) prevents `FTestLogCapture` from capturing the line, (b) prevents subsequent ticks from publishing the inactive snapshot, and (c) breaks the "publish inactive for app lifetime" guarantee. All previously-Fatal callsites use `UE_LOG(..., Error, ...) + ensureAlways(false)` instead — Error is captured and `ensureAlways` breakpoints in Debug/Development without termination.

    **Null-at-tick-time defensive policy**: If a curve reference becomes null at tick time (post-initialization — a bug, not a supported state, e.g., from an editor hot-reload that orphaned the reference), DPC logs `LogDPC, Error` once per null occurrence per curve (not per tick) and **publishes the inactive snapshot for that tick** (`is_active = false`, all scalars at their inactive-snapshot values per the States and Transitions section). The previous per-scalar-floor fallback was incoherent — `WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S=0.25s` is max density, `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S=0.70s` (R10d-bound; R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18) is min telegraph, `MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR=1` is min concurrency — they point in opposite gameplay directions and produced a degenerate "max-rate spawn with min telegraph but only 1 concurrent wave" state matching no designed phase. The inactive snapshot is a coherent safe state. Consumers see `is_active = false` and discard any phase-diff signal per Rule 16. The underlying bug must still be fixed; the inactive snapshot is the panic mode, not the recovery.

    **NaN/infinity defensive policy** (input-side guard, pre-division): If `remaining_time` (the input to F-1, read from RSM via `IRSMTimeStateProvider::GetRemainingTime()`) is NaN or non-finite at tick time, DPC logs `LogDPC, Error` once per occurrence per category (NaN / `+Inf` / `-Inf`), short-circuits F-1 (does NOT evaluate `(RUN_DURATION_S - remaining_time) / RUN_DURATION_S`), and publishes the inactive snapshot for that tick. The canonical guard is **at the top of DPC's tick function before F-1 evaluation**:

    A post-`clamp` `ensure(!FMath::IsNaN(t_norm) ...)` is **NOT** a valid guard because `FMath::Clamp<double>(NaN, 0.0, 1.0)` returns `1.0` (NaN comparisons evaluate false, both branches take the `Max` arm); `clamp(+Inf, 0, 1)` returns `1.0`; `clamp(-Inf, 0, 1)` returns `0.0`. The post-clamp value is always finite, so the post-clamp guard is unreachable for the cases it claims to catch. The guard must be at the input side.

15. **Snapshot atomicity is by construction (single-thread)**. DPC's tick and all named downstream consumers (Wave Spawner, Telegraph System, Pull-Wave Behavior, Audio Controller, Death Replay) execute on the game thread. The `FDPCFrameState` snapshot is therefore consistent by construction — a single-threaded write/read sequence cannot produce a fragmented snapshot. **Enforcement**: `GetCurrentFrameState()` and `GetPreviousFrameState()` open with `check(IsInGameThread())` as their first statement. The check is zero-cost in shipping builds (the macro compiles out) and catches off-thread access immediately in Debug/Development — a future programmer attempting to read DPC from the audio thread (UE's `FAudioThread` runs sound generators on a separate thread) or from a Niagara CPU sim callback will hit the assertion on the first off-thread call rather than producing a heisenbug from an unsynchronized read. If a future system needs legitimate off-thread read access, it must be added to this list AND the snapshot publication must be promoted to an atomic pointer swap (`std::atomic<TSharedPtr<FDPCFrameState>>` or equivalent) AND `GetCurrentFrameState()`'s thread guard must be relaxed accordingly. As of this GDD, no such consumer exists.

### States and Transitions

Three phases. Transition is purely a function of `t_norm` crossing a normalized threshold.

| Phase | `t_norm` range | At default 60s run | Felt experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OPENER` | `[0.0, OPENER_END_NORMALIZED)` | `(40.0, 60.0]s` remaining | One wave in flight. Generous telegraph. Learning the rhythm. |
| `MID` | `[OPENER_END_NORMALIZED, PEAK_START_NORMALIZED)` | `(15.0, 40.0]s` remaining | Waves begin to overlap. Telegraph tightens. The storm is gaining intelligence. |
| `PEAK` | `[PEAK_START_NORMALIZED, 1.0]` | `[0.0, 15.0]s` remaining | Up to 3 concurrent waves in-flight (spawned-and-traveling); telegraph onsets land edge-to-edge sequential at floor cadence in the default case (per-onset window 0.70s, stagger floor 0.70s → no overlap; R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — was 0.6s/0.6s pre-R10d). 1–3 barrage events per PEAK cluster M=3 onsets within W≤0.3s. Survival = pattern literacy + barrage triage. |

Per-phase default target values (anchor points; designer-authored curves interpolate between):

| Output | OPENER target | MID target | PEAK target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wave_spawn_interval_s` | `4.0` | `2.0` | `1.0` | Mean seconds between spawn attempts. |
| `telegraph_window_s` | `0.94` | `0.82` | `0.70` | PEAK value equals the runtime floor — no slack. **R12 game-designer BLOCKING-2 closure 2026-06-18**: per-phase default target values updated from pre-R10d `0.9/0.72/0.6` to CD-ruled Path B values `0.94/0.82/0.70` (PEAK = FLOOR; MID = FLOOR + 0.12s overlap; OPENER = MID + 0.12s overlap). R11a-arithmetic missed this anchor table; R12 corrects in lockstep with Tuning Knobs row at line 717 and AC-07/AC-09/canonical fixture at lines 782/800-801/820. |
| `max_concurrent_waves` | `1` | `2` | `3` | Subject to Pull-Wave Behavior thermal validation; `2` is the safe fallback for PEAK if mobile thermal budget fails. |

**Transition rules**:

- A phase transition happens on the **first tick** in which `t_norm` crosses the threshold value upward. No per-tick hysteresis (`t_norm` is monotonic by RSM contract).
- The transition tick is observable to consumers by snapshot diff: `previous_snapshot.current_phase != current_snapshot.current_phase`.
- DPC does not pause, defer, or align the transition tick to any external event (such as a wave spawn cycle). **Rest-beat alignment is the Wave Spawner's responsibility** (see Interactions and Dependencies).
- Once the run leaves RUNNING or `is_paused` becomes `true`, DPC publishes the inactive snapshot on its next tick.

**Inactive snapshot** (published when RSM is not RUNNING or is paused, or when Rule 14's defensive policies fire):

```
FDPCFrameState {
    current_phase = ERunPhase::OPENER
    wave_spawn_interval_s = WaveSpawnIntervalCurve.GetFloatValue(0.0)
    telegraph_window_s    = max(TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S, TelegraphWindowCurve.GetFloatValue(0.0))
    max_concurrent_waves  = max(MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR, FMath::FloorToInt32(MaxConcurrentWavesCurve.GetFloatValue(0.0)))
    t_norm = 0.0
    is_active = false
}
```

The scalar values are identical to the run-start snapshot (so any consumer that ignores `is_active` still sees safe opening values), but the `is_active = false` flag tells consumers per Rule 16 to discard phase-diff signals across this snapshot. Active snapshots (published during RUNNING && !is_paused, including resume_grace per EC-2) have `is_active = true`.

**Inactive snapshot caching (performance)**: The inactive snapshot's scalar values are constant by construction after Rule 14 validation succeeds and curve assets are loaded (`t_norm = 0.0` always; curves are loaded once). DPC caches the inactive snapshot **only after curve assets are confirmed loaded**:

- **Synchronous curve load** (curves referenced directly in the DPC type's UPROPERTY, loaded by the cooker): caching runs at the end of `Initialize()` / `BeginPlay()` immediately after Rule 14 validation.
- **Asynchronous curve load** (curves loaded via `FStreamableHandle`): caching is **deferred to the `FStreamableHandle::CompleteDelegate` callback** (binding API form: `Handle->BindCompleteDelegate(FStreamableDelegate::CreateUObject(this, &UDPCController::OnCurvesLoaded))` — verify against pinned UE5.7 API at implementation time). Between `Initialize()` and the streamable-handle completion, every DPC tick publishes a **pre-init inactive snapshot** with hard-coded raw constants (R5 re-review 5 fix — closes performance-analyst R-1 / unreal-specialist R-1 floor-derived multiplier hazard): `current_phase = OPENER`, `wave_spawn_interval_s = INACTIVE_SNAPSHOT_WSI_DEFAULT = 4.0f` (literal — mirrors authored OPENER `WaveSpawnIntervalCurve` value at `t_norm=0.0`; NOT derived as `WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S * 16.0` because the multiplier 16 couples the pre-init value to the floor constant — if `WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S` is later tuned, the derived pre-init would diverge from the authored OPENER curve), `telegraph_window_s = INACTIVE_SNAPSHOT_TW_DEFAULT = 0.94f` (literal — mirrors authored OPENER `TelegraphWindowCurve` value at `t_norm=0.0` under Path B; NOT derived as `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S * 1.5` because under R10d FLOOR=0.70s that derivation produces 1.05s ≠ authored OPENER 0.94s; **R13 2026-06-19 oracle-site sweep correction — was `0.9f` literal + stale `0.9s/1.35s` prose example pre-R10d/Path-B; struct default at line 165 corrected in lock-step; ADR-0004 §oracle-site corollary extended with struct-default + pre-init-constant sub-classes**), `max_concurrent_waves = MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR = 1`, `t_norm = 0.0`, `is_active = false`. Both named constants are declared in DPC code with comments noting they must equal the authored OPENER curve values at `t_norm=0.0` (designer-facing invariant: if OPENER `WaveSpawnIntervalCurve` or `TelegraphWindowCurve` key at `t_norm=0.0` is re-authored, the pre-init constants must be updated to match). The pre-init snapshot is hard-coded rather than curve-evaluated because the curves are not yet valid. On streamable-handle completion, DPC runs Rule 14 validation, computes the canonical cached inactive snapshot from `WaveSpawnIntervalCurve.GetFloatValue(0.0)` etc., and replaces the pre-init snapshot atomically (single game-thread write).

The previous version of this paragraph said caching runs "at the end of `Initialize()`" unconditionally, which produced an async-load race: if curves were not yet loaded, the cached values would be derived from null curve refs (Rule 14's null-curve policy returns the inactive snapshot, but the cache would be permanently locked at those degraded values rather than recomputing once curves load). Deferring caching to `OnComplete` closes the race.

Caching eliminates three `GetFloatValue(0.0)` calls per non-RUNNING tick. The corrected lifecycle math: a 60-second run (60s active) + 30s score reveal (inactive: COMPLETE → RESOLVING) + ~10s menu return (inactive: IDLE) + ~5s COUNTDOWN (inactive) ≈ 105s cycle, of which 45s is inactive (~43% of ticks). The optimization is real but smaller than the prior `~80%` claim — the prior figure was wrong.

### Interactions with Other Systems

**Run State Machine (upstream)** — DPC reads `current_state`, `is_paused`, and `remaining_time` each tick. DPC sends nothing to RSM. DPC's tick must be ordered after RSM's tick within the same frame (bound by OQ-7 ADR).

**Wave Spawner & Pattern Library (downstream)** — Reads the published `FDPCFrameState` once per spawn decision. Uses `current_phase` to select the active pattern pool, `wave_spawn_interval_s` as the cadence target, `max_concurrent_waves` as the cap, and `is_active` as the Rule 16 gate. Detects phase transitions by diffing `current_phase` against its prior-frame cache (or via `DPC.GetPreviousFrameState()`); on a transition gated by Rule 16, Wave Spawner swaps pattern pool atomically. **Cross-system contract (binding)**: Wave Spawner's pattern library follows the **spawn drain** mechanism — within `max_pattern_length_s` of a phase boundary, no new pattern is started; in-flight patterns complete naturally. This produces a 1–4s rest beat before each phase transition. **Rationale (corrected attribution)**: the rest beat is an operational consequence of disallowing pattern straddling at phase boundaries — it ensures the pool swap is clean (no leftover patterns from the prior pool active when the new pool engages). The rest beat is acceptable, and arguably aids pacing, but it is NOT a Pillar 5 mechanism (Pillar 5 is about death-attribution / no-mystery-RNG; a timer-driven silence cannot serve a skill-visibility pillar). Earlier wording attributed the rest beat to Pillar 5 — that attribution was incorrect and has been removed. The pacing benefit is real; the pillar justification is operational simplicity. The new phase's pool engages on the next spawn decision after the boundary. See "Wave Spawner Contract Definitions" below for the full vocabulary (pattern, spawn cycle, pattern pool, drain mechanism, pool swap timing, asymmetric drain exposure, per-phase pattern-length caps, run-termination drain, intra-pattern telegraph stagger). Wave Spawner GDD must implement and include corresponding ACs.

**Telegraph System (downstream)** — Reads `telegraph_window_s` from the snapshot when computing telegraph lead time. Not concerned with the phase enum directly.

**Pull-Wave Behavior (downstream)** — Reads `current_phase` if pull velocity/behavior varies by phase (TBD in Pull-Wave GDD; this GDD anticipates the read). May also read `max_concurrent_waves` for self-limiting checks.

**Audio Controller (downstream)** — Reads `current_phase`; detects transitions by snapshot diff and fires phase-transition stings/layer crossfades on diff. Does not read scalars. Tempo and music intensity are driven by Audio Controller's own per-phase mix definition keyed off `current_phase`.

**Death Replay** — Reads the captured per-tick `FDPCFrameState` from the replay buffer at playback time. Because DPC is deterministic and pure-function on `remaining_time`, replay reconstructs the curve exactly from the captured RSM time stream.

**Scoring Logic** — Does not read DPC. (Open question: should peak-phase slips be worth more? Captured in Open Questions.)

**Camera, In-Run HUD, End-Run Screen, Score Persistence, Near-Miss Detection, Input System** — Do not read DPC.

## Formulas

#### F-1: Normalized Progress

```
t_norm = clamp((RUN_DURATION_S − remaining_time) / RUN_DURATION_S, 0.0, 1.0)
```

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RUN_DURATION_S` | `double` (seconds) | `(0.0, ∞)` | Total run length; RSM default `60.0`, tunable in `[10, 300]` |
| `remaining_time` | `double` (seconds) | `[0.0, RUN_DURATION_S]` | Read from RSM; monotonically non-increasing during RUNNING; frozen while `is_paused` |
| `t_norm` | `double` | `[0.0, 1.0]` | Normalized run progress; `0.0` = run start, `1.0` = run end |

**Output Range:** `[0.0, 1.0]` (both ends clamped). `clamp` is a safety net against RSM ticks arriving before the first frame or floating-point overshoot at run end.

**Valid only when:** RSM `current_state == RUNNING` and `is_paused == false`. In other states, F-1 is not evaluated; DPC publishes the inactive snapshot.

**Example:** `remaining_time = 45.0`, `RUN_DURATION_S = 60.0` → `t_norm = (60.0 − 45.0) / 60.0 = 0.25` (in OPENER phase).

**Boundary checks:**

| Input condition | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `remaining_time = RUN_DURATION_S` (first RUNNING tick) | `t_norm = 0.0` | Pre-clamp value is exactly `0.0` |
| `remaining_time = 0.0` (timer expired) | `t_norm = 1.0` | Pre-clamp value is exactly `1.0`; RSM transitions to COMPLETE on the same tick (DPC publishes inactive snapshot on the next) |
| `remaining_time > RUN_DURATION_S` (impossible per RSM but clamp guards) | `t_norm = 0.0` | Should never occur; clamp is the safety net |
| `remaining_time < 0.0` (impossible per RSM but clamp guards) | `t_norm = 1.0` | Should never occur; clamp is the safety net |

#### F-2: Phase Function

```
current_phase = OPENER if t_norm < OPENER_END_NORMALIZED
              = MID    if t_norm < PEAK_START_NORMALIZED
              = PEAK   otherwise
```

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `t_norm` | `double` | `[0.0, 1.0]` | Output of F-1 |
| `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` | `double` | `(0.0, PEAK_START_NORMALIZED)` | Upper bound of OPENER phase; default `0.333` |
| `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` | `double` | `(OPENER_END_NORMALIZED, 1.0)` | Lower bound of PEAK phase; default `0.75` |
| `current_phase` | `ERunPhase` | `{OPENER, MID, PEAK}` | Active phase enum |

**Output Range:** Discrete 3-value enum. Boundaries use strict `<`, so `t_norm = 0.333` falls in MID and `t_norm = 0.75` falls in PEAK.

**Valid only when:** `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED + MIN_MID_WIDTH_NORMALIZED <= PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` (with `MIN_MID_WIDTH_NORMALIZED = 0.10`) — asserted at startup per Rule 14's threshold-ordering precondition. AC-THRESHOLD-ORDER and AC-MIN-MID-WIDTH test the assertion.

**Example:** `t_norm = 0.25` → `OPENER`; `t_norm = 0.333` → `MID`; `t_norm = 0.80` → `PEAK`.

**Boundary checks:**

| Input condition | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `t_norm = OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` (exactly `0.333`) | `MID` | Strict `<` assigns the boundary tick to the entering phase — first MID tick is observable |
| `t_norm = PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` (exactly `0.75`) | `PEAK` | Strict `<` assigns the boundary tick to PEAK — first PEAK tick is observable |
| `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED ≥ PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` (misconfig) | Assert-fail at startup | MID phase has zero width; structural error |

**Output Range:** `[WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S, ∞)` in seconds. Curve typically decreases monotonically from OPENER to PEAK. DPC tolerates non-monotonic designer curves at runtime — startup validation logs a warning if non-monotonic (designer may intentionally author a mid-run "breather"; warning surfaces intent vs. accident).

**Example:** Curve at default anchors returns `4.0s` at `t_norm = 0.0`, `2.0s` at `t_norm = 0.333`, `1.0s` at `t_norm = 0.75`. Floor catches authoring errors that dip below `0.25s`.

**Boundary checks:**

| Input condition | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `t_norm = 0.0` | Curve at key `0`; ≥ floor | Longest interval (OPENER) |
| `t_norm = 1.0` | Curve at key `1`; ≥ floor | Shortest interval (PEAK); floor prevents ≤ 0 |
| Curve evaluates ≤ 0 | `WAVE_SPAWN_INTERVAL_FLOOR_S` (`0.25s`) | Without floor, `interval = 0` would trigger continuous spawning |
| Non-monotonic curve (dip then rise) | Accepted at runtime; startup warning | Intentional mid-run breather is legal; warning surfaces tuning intent |

##### F-3b: Telegraph Window

```
telegraph_window_s = max(TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S, TelegraphWindowCurve.GetFloatValue(t_norm))
```

**Output Range:** `[0.70, ∞)` in seconds (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — R10d-bound at 0.70s; was `[0.6, ∞)` PROVISIONAL pre-R10d). The PROVISIONAL marker now applies only to the empirical-confirmation phase per restructured Rule 9 two-phase gate; the design-time value is locked at 0.70s under R10d CD ruling path (i) (2026-06-17). The floor as a mechanism is structural to Pillar 2; the numeric is design-time bound under R10d.

**Example:** Curve at R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 production anchors returns `0.94s` at `t_norm = 0.0`, `0.82s` at `t_norm = 0.333`, `0.70s` at `t_norm = 0.75` (Path B reauthoring per R6 Q7 binding rule under R10d FLOOR=0.70s; pre-R10d keys were 0.9/0.72/0.6). A curve authoring error producing `0.4s` at PEAK is clamped to `0.70s`.

**Boundary checks:**

| Input condition | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `t_norm = 0.0` | Curve at key `0`; ≥ `0.70` | Generous window in OPENER (R11a-arithmetic — at production keys `(0.0, 0.94)`, returns 0.94s) |
| `t_norm = 1.0` | Curve at key `1`; ≥ `0.70` | PEAK compresses but floor holds (R11a-arithmetic — at production keys `(1.0, 0.70)`, returns 0.70s = FLOOR) |
| Curve `< 0.70s` anywhere | `0.70s` (clamped) | Pillar 2 invariant maintained at runtime (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — was 0.6s pre-R10d) |

##### F-3c: Max Concurrent Waves

```
max_concurrent_waves = FMath::FloorToInt32(FMath::Clamp(MaxConcurrentWavesCurve.GetFloatValue(t_norm), float(MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR), float(MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_CAP)))
```

**Output Range:** `[MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR, MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_CAP]` = `[1, 16]` as `int32`. Both bounds are enforced by clamping the float return value *before* `FMath::FloorToInt32` — the float-side clamp is required because `FMath::FloorToInt32` of a large-but-finite float (e.g., `1e12f`) is implementation-defined on ARM targets (the R4 performance-analyst BLOCKING finding on `FloorToInt32` finite-overflow). `MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_CAP = 16` is non-tunable and structural — it prevents a misconfigured curve from producing an unbounded integer while remaining well above the designed PEAK maximum of 3. `MAX_CONCURRENT_WAVES_FLOOR = 1` prevents a "dead run" with zero concurrent waves. **NaN/Inf input precondition**: Rule 8's output-side finite guard runs *before* `FMath::FloorToInt32` is called, so a NaN/Inf curve return value publishes the inactive snapshot and never reaches this clamp — eliminating the implementation-defined `static_cast<int32>(NaN)` UB.

**Startup warning: mid-phase integer crossing**. A designer-authored `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve` may step `max_concurrent_waves` mid-phase if its float value crosses an integer boundary strictly inside a phase's `t_norm` range (e.g., a key sequence `(0.5, 1.9), (0.6, 2.5), (0.7, 2.5)` makes the cap jump from `1` to `2` at the inflection point — a mid-MID density jump that fires no phase-diff signal and no consumer sting). This is a hidden difficulty bump from the Audio Controller's perspective (no signal) but a real density change from the player's perspective. DPC startup validation logs `LogDPC, Warning` if any integer floor-crossing in `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve` occurs strictly between `0.0` and `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED`, strictly between `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` and `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED`, or strictly between `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` and `1.0`. The warning surfaces accidental mid-phase density jumps; intentional ones can be acknowledged by suppressing the warning via a per-curve `bAllowMidPhaseConcurrencySteps` boolean on the curve asset's metadata (default false). Authoring intent is then explicit in source control.

**Example:** Curve at default anchors returns `1.0` at `t_norm = 0.0` → `max(1, floor(1.0)) = 1`; `2.0` at `t_norm = 0.333` → `2`; `3.0` at `t_norm = 0.75` → `3`. Thermal fallback (PEAK target `2` instead of `3`) is achieved by re-authoring `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve`'s key at `t_norm = 0.75` — no code change.

**Boundary checks:**

| Input condition | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `t_norm = 0.0` | `floor(curve[0])`; ≥ `1` | Expect `1` wave in OPENER |
| `t_norm = 1.0` | `floor(curve[1])`; ≥ `1` | Expect peak concurrency |
| Curve evaluates `< 1.0` (e.g., `0.5`) | `floor(0.5) = 0` → clamped to `1` | Without floor, `0` concurrent waves = no game |
| Curve evaluates negative | `float_clamp(neg, 1, 16) = 1` → `floor(1.0) = 1` | Float-side clamp lower-bound prevents `floor(neg) < 0` |
| Curve evaluates large-but-finite (e.g., `1e12`) | `float_clamp(1e12, 1, 16) = 16.0` → `floor(16.0) = 16` | Cap prevents implementation-defined ARM behavior in `FMath::FloorToInt32` for large floats (R4 BLOCKING fix) |

---

**Scalar continuity across phase boundaries (scoped to scalar values only — NOT system-level behavior):**

F-3a / F-3b / F-3c are purely curve-driven. The phase enum (F-2) is consumed by other systems but does **not** gate or modify scalar evaluation. Therefore all three scalars are continuous at `t_norm = 0.333` and `t_norm = 0.75` — no discontinuity in `wave_spawn_interval_s`, `telegraph_window_s`, or the pre-floor real value of `max_concurrent_waves` can arise from F-3 alone. The phase enum step is the only step at the boundary tick; downstream consumers observing the step do so by snapshot diff, not by detecting scalar discontinuity.

**Important**: This continuity invariant describes scalar **values**, not system-level **behavior**. Wave Spawner's pattern pool swaps atomically at the boundary tick (a behavioral discontinuity). Audio Controller's transition sting fires at the boundary tick (a behavioral discontinuity). Pull-Wave Behavior may change pull velocity at the boundary tick if it scales by phase (behavioral discontinuity). The scalar continuity prevents "scalar pop" (e.g., a sudden jump in telegraph window), which would be perceived as a glitch. It does NOT prevent the intentional system-level transitions that ARE the design's phase change. Downstream GDD authors should read this as "no unexpected scalar pop," not as "no behavioral discontinuity at boundary."

## Edge Cases

**EC-1: RUNNING → DEAD on the same tick DPC polls**
If RSM transitions `current_state` from RUNNING to DEAD during the tick that DPC evaluates (RSM Rule 17 evaluates `death_confirmed` before timer expiry, and the OQ-7 ADR places DPC's tick after RSM's): DPC reads `current_state == DEAD` and publishes the **inactive snapshot**. The death tick's difficulty scalars are not captured. Death Replay reconstructs the curve from the captured RSM time stream — not from per-tick DPC snapshots — so no information is lost. Existing Rule 4 handles it.

**EC-2: Resume grace window — DPC publishes active snapshot**
If RSM is `RUNNING` and `is_paused == false` but `resume_grace == true`: DPC's Rule 4 gate (`RUNNING && !is_paused`) is satisfied, so DPC **publishes an active snapshot** with the current `t_norm` and full scalars. DPC does not gate on `resume_grace`. Gameplay suppression during grace (no spawns, no collision, no movement) is the responsibility of Wave Spawner, Collision, and Player Movement — see RSM Rule 19. Were DPC to publish inactive during grace, a player returning from background mid-PEAK would see a spurious OPENER reset, corrupting Audio Controller's mix and Wave Spawner's pattern pool selection. DPC's contract is `t_norm` → scalars, not gameplay gating.

**EC-3: ABORTED mid-run**
If RSM transitions to ABORTED from RUNNING or COUNTDOWN (`PAUSED_TIMEOUT_S` exceeded on foreground): on the next DPC tick, DPC reads `current_state == ABORTED` and publishes the **inactive snapshot**. The last active snapshot (before the background that led to abort) is the final non-inactive snapshot DPC produced for that run. Death Replay discards its buffer on ABORTED (RSM Interactions), so no consumer needs a mid-abort active snapshot.

**EC-4: Tick-order violation — DPC ticks before RSM**
If the OQ-7 ADR resolution is misconfigured such that DPC ticks before RSM updates `remaining_time` for the current frame: DPC reads the previous frame's value, producing a one-frame-stale `t_norm`. Silent correctness violation — no crash, no assert, wrong output. **Resolution**: at DPC initialization, validate that RSM is registered as a tick prerequisite per the OQ-7 mechanism. If tick ordering cannot be statically verified (e.g., on a `UGameInstanceSubsystem` path), log `LogDPC, Warning` on the first RUNNING tick if `remaining_time` is identical to the prior tick's value — a runtime canary, not a fix. The OQ-7 ADR is the blocking remedy; this EC documents the symptom and detection path.

**EC-5: Frame hitch crossing a phase boundary**
If a frame hitch delays DPC's tick such that `t_norm` advances across a phase boundary between two consecutive DPC ticks (e.g., a 500ms hitch during the OPENER→MID crossing at `t_norm = 0.333`): DPC publishes the new phase on the first post-hitch tick. The snapshot diff `previous_snapshot.current_phase != current_snapshot.current_phase` fires exactly once — on that tick — observed correctly by all consumers. No transition is lost. The pathological case of skipping a phase entirely (OPENER → PEAK in one tick) requires `t_norm` to advance by more than `0.417` in one tick, which at minimum `RUN_DURATION_S = 10s` requires a >4-second frame hitch — outside design contract.

**EC-6: UCurveFloat key coverage at `t_norm` extremes**
If a designer authors a curve with keys only in the interior of `[0.0, 1.0]` (e.g., keys at `0.1` and `0.9`): UE's `UCurveFloat::GetFloatValue()` uses pre/post-infinity extrapolation (Constant, Linear, Cycle, etc., designer-configurable) — which may produce unexpected values at `t_norm = 0.0` or `1.0`. **Resolution**: per Rule 14, startup validation requires at least one key at or before `t_norm = 0.0` and at least one key at or after `t_norm = 1.0`. Extrapolation never fires inside DPC's evaluation domain; its setting becomes immaterial. Startup logs a warning if violated.

**EC-7: Construction and hot-reload spurious-diff prevention (NARROW SCOPE)**
At DPC construction, the previous-frame and current-frame snapshot fields are both initialized to the inactive default with `is_active == false`. On the first tick after construction, the previous-frame field overwrites with the *old* current value (the inactive default), then the current field overwrites with the new computed snapshot. A consumer that calls `GetPreviousFrameState()` and `GetCurrentFrameState()` immediately after construction (before the first tick) sees both equal to the inactive default — no diff, even with `is_active == false`. Editor hot-reload that reconstructs DPC's UObject is treated identically to construction: the post-reload state is `prev == current == inactive default`. **This contract is narrow by design** — it covers ONLY construction-time and hot-reload paths. It does **NOT** prevent spurious diffs on (a) pause-entry/exit (handled by Rule 16's `is_active` gate, EC-9 and EC-10) or (b) mid-run consumer subscription on a boundary tick (handled by Rule 17's consumer-side initialization protocol). The prior version of this EC claimed broader coverage; that claim was incorrect because the prev/current overwrite cycle progresses per-tick regardless of whether any consumer has subscribed, so the "first-call contract" only holds before any tick has executed.

**EC-9: Pause-entry — last RUNNING snapshot is active, first paused snapshot is inactive**
On the tick when RSM's `is_paused` becomes `true` (Rule 4's gate flips to inactive): DPC publishes the inactive snapshot with `is_active == false`. The previous-frame field holds the last RUNNING snapshot (e.g., PEAK with `is_active == true`). A naive Audio Controller diffing prev vs current would see `PEAK → OPENER` and fire a backwards transition sting — a Pillar 3 violation (the run-arc is corrupted by a phantom regression cue) and a Pillar 4 violation (a tonal escalation cue fires at a moment unrelated to the world's actual state). **Resolution**: Rule 16's `is_active` gate is the binding contract. Because `current_snapshot.is_active == false`, Audio Controller MUST update its local cache silently and NOT fire any sting. The pause-entry transition is suppressed by contract, not by DPC mechanism.

**EC-10: Pause-exit (including resume_grace) — first active snapshot after inactive sequence**
On the tick when RSM's `is_paused` becomes `false` and `current_state == RUNNING` (Rule 4's gate flips back to active, or EC-2's resume_grace path): DPC publishes the active snapshot with `is_active == true`. The previous-frame field holds the last paused snapshot (inactive, OPENER, `is_active == false`). A naive Audio Controller diff would see `OPENER → PEAK` (or whichever phase the run resumed into) and fire a misplaced sting at an arbitrary mid-PEAK moment — corrupting Pillar 3's emotional arc. **Resolution**: Rule 16's gate also catches this case because `previous_snapshot.is_active == false`. The diff is silenced; the consumer updates its cache and resumes normal diff behavior on the *next* tick (active → active).

**EC-11: `RUN_DURATION_S = 0` defensive guard**
If RSM exposes a misconfigured `RUN_DURATION_S = 0.0` (outside RSM's documented `[10, 300]s` range, reachable only via a config file edit or test stub bug): DPC's F-1 would divide by zero. Rule 14's initialization assertion `check(RUN_DURATION_S > 0.0)` catches this in development; in shipping builds (with `check` stripped), DPC's parallel runtime guard fires: `UE_LOG(LogDPC, Error, ...)` is captured AND `ensureAlways(false)` breakpoints in Debug/Development without terminating, AND DPC publishes the inactive snapshot for the entire app lifetime. The process continues running (no Fatal abort). Ownership: RSM owns the canonical `[10, 300]` range assertion (per its Tuning Knobs); DPC adds a defensive `> 0` guard because the failure mode (division by zero) is severe.

**EC-12: Tick-time NaN/non-finite `remaining_time` or `RUN_DURATION_S`**
If `remaining_time` or `RUN_DURATION_S` is NaN or `±Infinity` at tick time (a contract violation upstream — RSM's values should always be finite, but defensive case): per Rule 14's input-side NaN/infinity defensive policy, DPC logs `LogDPC, Error` once per occurrence per category (NaN / `+Inf` / `-Inf`), fires `ensureAlways(false)`, publishes the inactive snapshot for that tick, and skips F-1 entirely. The guard fires **before** F-1's division and clamp — the canonical guard placement is at the top of DPC's tick function, NOT after `t_norm` is computed. A post-clamp guard would be unreachable for NaN/Inf inputs because `FMath::Clamp<double>(NaN, 0.0, 1.0)` returns `1.0`, `clamp(+Inf, 0, 1)` returns `1.0`, and `clamp(-Inf, 0, 1)` returns `0.0` — all finite. See Rule 14's pseudocode for the canonical guard sequence. AC-NAN-GUARD validates this contract.

**EC-13: Async-load race — RSM enters RUNNING before DPC's curve assets complete loading**
If DPC's three `UCurveFloat` assets are loaded asynchronously via `FStreamableHandle` and `FStreamableHandle::CompleteDelegate` has not fired by the time RSM transitions from COUNTDOWN to RUNNING (a race: load completes after gameplay begins): DPC has not run Rule 14 validation and has not computed the cached inactive snapshot from real curve data. Every DPC tick during the load window publishes the pre-init inactive snapshot (`is_active = false`, hard-coded safe defaults — see Inactive Snapshot Caching under States and Transitions). Wave Spawner, Audio Controller, and all downstream consumers observe `is_active == false` and, per Rule 16, issue no spawns, stings, or mix changes. The player sees a blank RUNNING state — no pulls, no audio escalation, no gameplay — for the duration of the load window.

**Chosen mitigation: Bound-and-Abort** (timeout → ABORTED, R5 Cluster B Item 3, watchdog-gating refined R5 re-review 5, mechanism specified R6). DPC starts a `DPC_ASYNC_LOAD_WATCHDOG_S = 5.0s` watchdog **on RSM's transition from COUNTDOWN to RUNNING** (NOT at DPC `Initialize()`). The watchdog is deferred to RUNNING entry to eliminate the COUNTDOWN-overlap race: at Initialize(), the typical 5s COUNTDOWN absorbs most load time, and the watchdog should bound the *post-COUNTDOWN* spillover into RUNNING, not the COUNTDOWN+spillover combined.

**Watchdog implementation mechanism (R6 systems-designer B-2 + performance-analyst HIGH fix; R7 Path A refinement)**: the watchdog is **tick-driven**, not `FTimerManager`-driven. DPC accumulates `watchdog_elapsed_s += FMath::Min(DeltaTime, MAX_WATCHDOG_DELTA_CLAMP_S)` inside its own `Tick(float DeltaTime)` body — **inside the `!bInitialized` pre-init path per R7 Path A** (the engine ticks DPC during async load because `IsTickable()` returns true unconditionally; the pre-init path is responsible for both publication and watchdog advancement). The `FMath::Min` clamp (R7 performance-analyst R-3 fix) prevents a single GC stall or shader-compile spike from consuming the entire watchdog budget in one frame: `MAX_WATCHDOG_DELTA_CLAMP_S = 0.1s` (= one frame at 10Hz, conservative; the watchdog measures load-progress time, not wall-clock). This produces three required behaviors:
- **Pause-freeze**: Rule 4's `is_paused == true` gate causes DPC's pre-init path to skip watchdog accumulation when RSM is paused (the pre-init path checks RSM state before advancing the accumulator: only advance when `RSM->GetCurrentState() == ERunState::RUNNING && !RSM->GetIsPaused()`). The watchdog naturally freezes during pause.
- **Background-freeze**: `IsTickableWhenPaused()` returns `false` (per Rule 3 overrides) AND mobile UE5 suspends FTickableGameObject ticks during app backgrounding via `FCoreDelegates::ApplicationWillDeactivateDelegate` → `ApplicationHasReactivatedDelegate`. The watchdog accumulator does not advance during background — the player can receive a notification, background the app for arbitrary wall-clock time, and return to find the watchdog at the same elapsed value (eliminating accidental run-termination from background notifications, a critical mobile UX concern).
- **Cancellation on validation completion (R7 systems-designer B-2 fix — user Q2=A)**: when `FStreamableHandle::CompleteDelegate` fires and Rule 14 validation **concludes** (success OR failure path), DPC sets `bInitialized = true`. The watchdog evaluation path begins with `if (bInitialized) { watchdog_elapsed_s = 0.0; return; }` — preventing the post-init race where load completes at t=4.9s, validation takes 50ms, and the watchdog evaluation at t=4.95s aborts a successfully-initialized DPC, AND preventing the double-abort race where cap-vs-barrage failure issues `RequestAbort(ThermalFallbackBarrageIncompatible)` at t=3.0s but `bInitialized` stays false (under the prior R6 semantic), allowing the watchdog to fire a second `RequestAbort(AsyncLoadTimeout)` at t=5.0s. Under the R7 semantic, `bInitialized` means "validation concluded" not "validation succeeded"; the watchdog cancellation gate fires correctly on the failure path. Cancellation is implicit in this gate; no separate `bWatchdogActive` flag is required.

If `CompleteDelegate` has not fired within 5s of RUNNING entry (5s of *foreground unpaused tick time*, NOT wall-clock):
- DPC sets an internal fault flag, logs `LogDPC, Error` once (non-shipping: also `ensureAlways(false)`), and holds the pre-init inactive snapshot permanently for this run.
- DPC signals the abort trigger via the cross-system contract below.
- RSM transitions to ABORTED, which then transitions to IDLE per its normal ABORTED path. The "run interrupted" ceremony plays (downstream UI + Audio Controller own this, per RSM Player Fantasy).

**Early-warning tier (performance-analyst R5 re-review 5 recommendation)**: at 2.0s into the watchdog window, if `CompleteDelegate` has not fired, DPC emits a single `LogDPC, Warning` line with elapsed-since-RUNNING time. This gives developer telemetry on slow-load paths without changing player-facing behavior. Constant: `DPC_ASYNC_LOAD_WATCHDOG_WARN_S = 2.0s`.

**RUNNING-entry detection mechanism**: DPC's tick (Rule 4 gate) observes RSM's `current_state` each tick. The watchdog start is triggered on the first tick where `RSM->GetCurrentState() == ERunState::RUNNING && !RSM->GetIsPaused()` AND `bWatchdogStarted == false`. After watchdog start, `bWatchdogStarted = true` for the run; the flag clears on `IDLE` re-entry.

If `CompleteDelegate` fires within the watchdog window (the normal case — load completes during COUNTDOWN or early RUNNING), DPC runs Rule 14 validation, computes the real cached inactive snapshot, and on the next RUNNING tick with `is_active` requirements satisfied, begins publishing active snapshots normally. A brief `is_active = false` window at RUNNING entry is therefore expected and not a bug; its duration equals the remaining load time after RUNNING entry.

**Cross-system contract this EC imposes**: DPC must expose an `IsInitialized() const → bool` query (returns `true` after `CompleteDelegate` fires and Rule 14 validation passes). The abort-trigger mechanism is: DPC calls an injected `IDPCAbortDelegate::RequestAbort(reason: EDPCAbortReason::AsyncLoadTimeout)` interface at watchdog expiry; this interface's production implementation forwards the abort request to RSM (which transitions to ABORTED per its Rule 7 contract). The `IDPCAbortDelegate` is injected at DPC construction alongside `IRSMTimeStateProvider*` — it does not create a new RSM coupling direction (DPC → RSM) in the snapshot-read sense; it uses a one-way abort request. **OQ-7 note**: this interface is agnostic to RSM's UE object type — the production implementation of `IDPCAbortDelegate` handles the UE object type resolution internally, so DPC's abort path is valid across all four OQ-7 outcomes. **RSM behavior this EC depends on**: RSM must accept an external abort request mid-RUNNING and transition to ABORTED. If a future RSM ADR restricts ABORTED entry to PAUSED_TIMEOUT_S-only triggers, this dependency must be renegotiated. AC-ASYNC-LOAD-RACE validates this contract.

**EC-8: Flat curves (zero variation across `t_norm`)**
If all three curves are authored as horizontal lines (constant value across the entire `t_norm` domain): DPC produces valid snapshots every tick. F-2 is independent of scalar values, so the phase enum still steps at `OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` and `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` — downstream consumers still observe phase transitions and may fire stings or swap pattern pools. Scalars are constant throughout the run. This is a **legal degenerate case** — a mechanically flat run arc with no difficulty escalation, violating Pillar 3 *experientially* but not crashing. Acceptable for automated testing fixtures; not acceptable for shipped content. Tuning Knobs section documents this as a designer-error class, not a runtime fault.

**Wave Spawner Contract Definitions** (binding terms inherited by Wave Spawner GDD):

- **Pattern**: a composed sequence of one or more sub-spawn events authored as a single unit (e.g., "two waves left then one right with a 0.4s gap"). A pattern has a defined `pattern_length_s` from the first sub-spawn to the last sub-spawn's expected completion (telegraph end). The Wave Spawner GDD will own the pattern data structure; the DPC contract only requires that `pattern_length_s` is a known field on each pattern asset.

- **Spawn cycle**: a single pattern's execution from start (first sub-spawn issued) to end (last sub-spawn completes). A pattern is either authored as one-shot (single cycle) or as a looped sequence (multiple cycles until cancelled). "Mid-spawn-cycle" in the rest-beat contract means "between a cycle's start and end."

- **Pattern pool (binding: pre-authored static set; jointly owned)**: Each phase owns a hand-authored named list of pattern assets (e.g., `OPENER_PATTERNS = [intro_solo, gentle_pair, slow_arc, ...]`, `MID_PATTERNS = [...]`, `PEAK_PATTERNS = [...]`). The spawner selects patterns from the current phase's list using a Wave Spawner-owned selection rule subject to the constraints in the "Pattern selection rule" item below. **Joint-ownership note**: this MVP-scoping decision (static set rather than procedural/dynamic) lives in this DPC GDD for historical reasons (it was bound in DPC's first review when Wave Spawner GDD had no author yet). It is structurally a Wave Spawner concern; when Wave Spawner GDD is authored, it MUST re-declare this scoping decision in its own MVP section and the two GDDs must agree. If Wave Spawner's MVP section diverges from this binding, the divergence is a GDD-level conflict that triggers `/propagate-design-change`. Procedural pattern generation and dynamic queues are explicitly out of scope for MVP under both GDDs' definitions.

- **Drain mechanism (binding: spawn drain)**: As `t_norm` approaches a phase boundary (`OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` or `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED`), Wave Spawner stops issuing NEW pattern starts within `max_pattern_length_s` of the boundary, where `max_pattern_length_s` is the per-phase cap (OPENER ≤ 2.0s, MID ≤ 3.0s, PEAK uncapped). In-flight patterns that began before the cutoff complete naturally — their final sub-spawns may fire just before the boundary tick or at the boundary tick itself, but no NEW pattern is started inside the drain window. The result is a 1–3s rest beat before each phase transition. **Rationale**: this is operational simplicity — the drain guarantees the pool swap is clean (no leftover patterns from the prior pool active when the new pool engages). The rest beat is acceptable and aids pacing, but it is NOT a Pillar 5 mechanism (Pillar 5 is about death-attribution / no-mystery-RNG; a timer-driven silence does not serve a skill-visibility pillar). Pillar 3 (run-arc legibility) covers the pacing benefit.

- **Per-phase pattern-length caps (binding)**: `max_pattern_length_s` is bounded per phase to protect against degenerate drain windows. OPENER ≤ 2.0s, MID ≤ 3.0s, PEAK uncapped (PEAK has no closing boundary; patterns extending into run-termination are governed by the run-termination drain clause below). The caps are validated by Wave Spawner asset-authoring tooling at content-cook time: any OPENER pattern with `pattern_length_s > 2.0` fails the cook with a tooling error. Without caps, a designer-authored long pattern could consume most of a short-`RUN_DURATION_S` phase (at `RUN_DURATION_S = 10s` with default fractions, OPENER is 3.33s wall-clock; a 6s OPENER pattern would force the drain window to exceed OPENER itself).

- **Pool swap timing**: When `current_phase` steps at the boundary tick (Rule 7), Wave Spawner's active pool switches atomically from the prior phase's list to the new phase's list on the next spawn decision after the boundary. Because of the drain mechanism, there are no in-flight patterns from the prior pool when the swap happens; the new phase's first pattern starts cleanly.

- **Asymmetric drain exposure**: At default `RUN_DURATION_S = 60s` with `max_pattern_length_s` of `2s/3s` (per the per-phase caps above) for OPENER and MID respectively, OPENER's 20s window loses ~10% to drain (effective ~18s of new-pattern starts), MID's 25s loses ~12% (effective ~22s), PEAK's 15s has no closing drain (no further phase boundary). Wave Spawner GDD must size per-phase pattern lists accounting for these effective windows. **Variety floors (Alpha-binding, R6 raises)**: OPENER ≥ 4 distinct patterns (raised R6 from ≥3 — level-designer FLAG-3), MID ≥ 4, PEAK ≥ 5 non-barrage. The OPENER raise extends cross-run recognition cycle from K=3 (stale by run 4) to K=4 (extends novelty to run 4–5), supporting onboarding for new players who haven't yet reliably reached MID/PEAK. PEAK's higher floor compensates for (a) no closing drain on its effective window and (b) highest spawn density (`wave_spawn_interval_s = 1.0s` at PEAK = ~15 pattern starts per run). The 4/4/5 ladder is provisional and may be revised after first playtest data lands; the *direction* (PEAK ≥ MID ≥ OPENER non-barrage) is binding.

  **Pool composition addition under Option B (R6 floor raise: barrage ≥3 → ≥4 for Path-B reauthoring margin — level-designer FLAG-2)**: Each pattern asset in the PEAK pool carries an `is_barrage: bool` field (default `false`). Patterns with `is_barrage=false` follow the default intra-pattern stagger rule; patterns with `is_barrage=true` are barrage patterns eligible for the carve-out (see Intra-pattern telegraph stagger constraint below). The PEAK variety floor now splits: OPENER ≥ 4 distinct patterns (R6 raise), MID ≥ 4 distinct patterns (unchanged), **PEAK ≥ 5 non-barrage (`is_barrage=false`) + ≥ 4 barrage (`is_barrage=true`) = ≥ 9 PEAK patterns total** (Alpha-binding, R6). Without the ≥4 floor, one Path-B per-pattern failure collapses the effective pool below the structural minimum and requires a mid-Alpha content-lock content authoring sprint. The ≥4 floor also widens the cross-run barrage memorization cycle to K ≤ 4 runs (was K ≤ 1.5 at ≥3 floor with target N=2 average) — improves Pillar 5 cross-run-recognition headroom. **Note for producer**: PEAK floor revised from ≥5 (pre-Option-B) → ≥7 (R5 Cluster A) → ≥8 (R5 re-review 5) → ≥9 (R6) total. - **Pattern selection rule (constraint, mechanism delegated)**: The mechanism (random / weighted / sequenced / shuffle-bag / etc.) is Wave Spawner GDD's choice. The DPC-binding *behavioral constraints* are: (1) no consecutive same-pattern repeats within a single phase exposure (a player who survives the OPENER→MID transition does not see two identical MID patterns back-to-back); (2) each pattern in the pool is seen at least once per K runs where K ≤ pool_size (so cross-run pattern recognition is achievable per Pillar 5 — every run distributes exposure across the full pool over time, not just a favored subset); (3) **PEAK barrage distribution** (Option B addition, R6 enforcement-binding): the selection rule MUST produce `BARRAGE_EVENTS_PER_PEAK_MIN` (= 1) ≤ N ≤ `BARRAGE_EVENTS_PER_PEAK_MAX` (= 3) barrage events per PEAK window, with target average ≈ `BARRAGE_EVENTS_PER_PEAK_TARGET_AVG` (= 2).
  - **Lower bound N ≥ 1 (structural guarantee)**: not satisfiable by a purely probabilistic rule. The selection mechanism MUST structurally guarantee at least one barrage selection per PEAK, for example by: pre-seeding the PEAK shuffle-bag with ≥1 `is_barrage=true` slot before randomizing the remainder, or by reserving the first PEAK selection cycle for a barrage pattern.
  - **Upper bound N ≤ 3 (structural enforcement — R6 BLOCKING fix, game-designer Q6 / level-designer B-1; R7 reset-semantics clarifier — systems-designer R-1)**: the prior version stated `N ≤ 3` as design intent without enforcement. Without a counter, expected barrages per PEAK at default tuning ≈ 1 + 14×(3/9) ≈ 5.67 (with shuffle-bag refills), silently exceeding the ceiling. **Required mechanism**: Wave Spawner maintains a `peak_barrage_count` counter, incremented on each barrage admission (NOT on deferred-queue insert), reset to 0 on PEAK entry (`current_phase` transitions to PEAK gated by Rule 16 — specifically, on the phase-diff handler firing the OPENER/MID→PEAK transition; NOT on subsequent Rule 16 `is_active` rising edges within the same PEAK exposure, e.g., pause-then-resume within PEAK does not reset the counter because `current_phase` doesn't change). When `peak_barrage_count == BARRAGE_EVENTS_PER_PEAK_MAX (3)`, all subsequent `is_barrage=true` candidates for the remainder of this PEAK are converted to non-barrage replacement draws (from the non-barrage shadow list — see below). The reserved barrage slot in the shuffle-bag continues to exist for the *next* PEAK; this PEAK's cap is enforced regardless of bag state. Counter scope is per-PEAK, per-run. **Admission semantics (R7 game-designer REC-1)**: the counter reflects admitted barrage patterns (admission = `available_slots ≥ required_slots` check passes), not player-perceived barrage resolution events. For BLOCKING purposes (N≤3 ceiling enforcement) this distinction is immaterial — any admitted barrage is a completed design decision regardless of whether the run ends during its execution.
  - **No consecutive barrage repeats**: constraint (1) no-consecutive-same-pattern applies; additionally, no two consecutive patterns in the same PEAK window may both be `is_barrage=true` (to prevent accidental barrage clusters that read as steady-state barrage, violating Phase Identity).
  - **Cross-run barrage distribution**: barrage_pool_size ≥ 4 (from variety floor R6 raise), so K ≤ 4 runs completes a cycle across all barrage patterns — ensures playtesters see different barrage configurations within the first 4 PEAK exposures with margin for AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE Path B per-pattern rejection.
  - **Mechanism examples that satisfy all constraints**: shuffle-bag with ≥1 reserved `is_barrage` slot + no-consecutive-same check + no-consecutive-barrage check + N≤3 counter + shadow-list replacement. A purely uniform random mechanism violates (1) (consecutive repeats possible), (2) (long-tail distribution), and (3) (cannot guarantee N≥1 and cannot enforce N≤3 without the counter) and is NOT compliant.

- **Shadow-list replacement mechanism (R6 BLOCKING fix — level-designer B-2; R7 merged-stream scope fix — systems-designer B-3)**: When the drop rule or the N≤3 counter requires drawing a "non-barrage replacement" for the spawn slot, the replacement is drawn from a **separate non-barrage shadow list**, NOT by advancing the shuffle-bag cursor. The shadow list contains the same non-barrage patterns as the main pool but is independent — drawing from the shadow list does NOT advance the shuffle-bag's draw-without-replacement state. **Rationale**: the prior version of the drop rule consumed shuffle-bag draws on every replacement, silently inflating K (cross-run distribution constraint (2)) and depleting the bag faster than the cross-run cycle requires. Under heavy deferral pressure, the bag could exhaust mid-PEAK and trigger an unintended reshuffle that re-seeded the reserved barrage slot (creating a 4th barrage opportunity even with the N≤3 counter fix). The shadow list is a uniform-random draw from non-barrage patterns with the no-consecutive-same constraint applied. **R7 scope fix (BLOCKING)**: the no-consecutive-same check on the shadow list MUST compare against the **most recently admitted pattern from EITHER source (main bag OR shadow list)**, NOT only against the shadow list's local prior draw. Without this scope, the following violation is reachable: main bag admits pattern X in one barrage cycle → next barrage cycle the N≤3 ceiling fires → shadow-list draw is taken → shadow list's local last-draw is null/different (not X) → shadow draws X → merged spawn stream reads …X, X… — consecutive same-pattern repeats, violating Pattern selection rule constraint (1) "no consecutive same-pattern repeats within a single phase exposure." The shared "most-recently-admitted pattern" state lives in Wave Spawner's spawn-decision module and is read by both the main-bag draw and the shadow-list draw. The shadow list still has no cross-run distribution obligation (replacements are not load-bearing for Pillar 5 pattern recognition — the player's regular bag draws already provide that cycle). Wave Spawner GDD specifies the shadow-list draw mechanism in its own rules section, including the merged-stream no-consecutive-same scope.

- **Spatial separation in barrage authoring (R6 BLOCKING fix — level-designer B-3; R7 BLOCKING fix — level-designer R7-B-1 spatial-variety collapse, CD-bound)**: barrage carve-out validation requires a numeric floor on spatial separation between concurrent telegraph onsets within a barrage. At 4-lane layouts the constraint is structurally unauthorable (no valid 3-onset configuration exists). CD adjudicated lowering to provisional=1 to restore spatial authorability at any plausible mobile lane count, and pushed the legibility validation to Pull-Wave GDD where the spatial content is actually authored. Interpretation at provisional=1: in a barrage pattern with M=3 concurrent onsets, lane assignment must place onsets such that any two onsets are in **different** lanes (no two onsets share a lane); adjacent lanes are permitted. If Pull-Wave's playtest data shows spatial-legibility failures at provisional=1, the floor raises to 2 (or higher) AND lane count raises in parallel to preserve ≥4 spatial variety — both adjustments are required together, not separately. Wave Spawner cook-time tooling enforces `MIN_BARRAGE_LANE_SEPARATION` on every `is_barrage=true` pattern in the PEAK pool; below-floor patterns fail cook with a tooling error.

- **Run-termination drain (binding)**: On RSM transition out of RUNNING (DEAD, COMPLETE, ABORTED), in-flight patterns continue their telegraph + slip resolution to natural end. The `is_active = false` snapshot gates the spawner from issuing NEW patterns post-transition (Rule 16). In-flight collision hits that resolve after the RSM transition are processed by Collision (Collision GDD owns the post-RUNNING hit policy); their gameplay effect is bookkept against the run that just ended, not a new run. This is symmetric with the phase-boundary spawn drain (no new patterns, in-flight completes) but applied at the terminal boundary instead of phase boundaries. Without this clause, Wave Spawner would have to invent terminal-state behavior with three plausible options (freeze immediately / allow completion / suppress hazard effects); leaving it unspecified risks divergent implementations.

  **Post-COMPLETE hit forward contract on Collision GDD (R6 CD-BOUND — closes level-designer FLAG-1)**: A PEAK barrage admitted near `remaining_time = 0` (e.g., barrage at t=59.5s with last onset at t=59.8s and telegraph completing at t=60.4s) can land collision hits AFTER RSM has transitioned RUNNING→COMPLETE at t=60.0s. RSM's state machine has NO COMPLETE→DEAD backward arc (single-terminal invariant), so an in-flight hit during RESOLVING state would otherwise produce an undefined outcome. **CD-bound resolution: Collision GDD MUST specify that post-RUNNING in-flight hits are scored (recorded in the run's hit log for Death Replay and Scoring) but CANNOT flip the run's terminal outcome**. A run that reached COMPLETE remains COMPLETE; a barrage hit landing at t=60.3s does not retroactively reclassify the run as DEAD. The hit is bookkept in the run that just ended (per the run-termination drain clause above) and may affect score totals if Scoring Logic chooses to count post-RUNNING hits, but the RSM terminal state is immutable post-transition. This binding is added here at DPC because the post-COMPLETE-hit hazard is structurally created by DPC's max_pattern_length_s caps allowing barrages to extend into RESOLVING; Collision GDD inherits the contract when authored. Alternative considered (cancel patterns whose `last_onset + TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S > RUN_DURATION_S` at COMPLETE transition) was rejected by CD because it silently changes Phase Identity in the last ~0.70s of every run (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — was ~0.6s pre-R10d) — players would notice barrages "missing" from the very end of PEAK.

- **Intra-pattern telegraph stagger constraint (binding, with barrage carve-out)**: Within any single pattern, no two telegraph onsets may occur within `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S` of each other. At the R10d-bound 0.70s floor (R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 — was PROVISIONAL 0.6s pre-R10d), the default constraint is "≥0.70s between any two telegraph-onset events in the same pattern." This prevents authoring a DPC-contract-compliant pattern that is Pillar-2-unsurvivable: 3 simultaneous telegraph onsets would force the player to triage 3 independent hazards in a sub-floor effective reaction window. The constraint converts an AC-failure risk into a content-tooling validation that surfaces at asset-cook time rather than playtest. The constraint is on telegraph *onsets*, not telegraph *durations* — overlapping telegraphs are allowed (3 telegraphs in flight simultaneously, each 0.70s, staggered ≥0.70s apart at onset, means the third telegraph starts as the first ends — DPC's `max_concurrent_waves = 3` is satisfied without simultaneous onset triage).

  **Barrage carve-out** (Option B — CD-ratified, Alpha-conditional): a pattern asset carrying the `is_barrage: bool = true` field (see Pool Composition above) AND residing in the **PEAK pattern pool only** MAY author up to `MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE` (= 3) pull onsets within `BARRAGE_SIMULTANEITY_WINDOW_S` (= 0.3s PROVISIONAL) of each other. The carve-out is explicitly **not admissible** in OPENER or MID pools; Wave Spawner asset-authoring tooling MUST reject any `is_barrage=true` pattern assigned to OPENER or MID at content-cook time (cook error, not warning). Barrage carve-out validity is conditional on **AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE** (Alpha-blocking playtest gate — see Acceptance Criteria). If AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE fails systemically, M and W must be revised and the Player Fantasy + Phase Identity sections must be re-bound under CD sign-off. Wave Spawner asset-authoring tooling SHOULD validate the default stagger constraint at cook time and SHOULD enforce barrage carve-out bounds (onset count ≤ M, onset spacing ≤ W); runtime enforcement is not required (assets are read-only).

- **Cross-pattern stagger policy (binding — closes R4 B2 cross-pattern stagger gap)**: When Wave Spawner is considering starting a new pattern with one or more patterns already in flight, the onset timing of the new pattern relative to the most recent onset of any in-flight pattern must satisfy a stagger floor. This policy is enforced at **spawn-decision time** (runtime, inside Wave Spawner's tick or spawn-cycle evaluation), NOT at cook time (which can only inspect individual pattern assets in isolation). Two cases:
  - **Non-barrage concurrent patterns** (neither the in-flight pattern nor the candidate new pattern is `is_barrage=true`): the candidate's first telegraph onset must be ≥ `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S` (R10d-bound 0.70s — R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18; was PROVISIONAL 0.6s pre-R10d) after the most recent telegraph onset of any in-flight pattern. This prevents cross-pattern simultaneous-onset triage for the default case — the same Pillar 2 guarantee the intra-pattern stagger constraint provides within a single pattern.
  - **Barrage-involved concurrent patterns** (either the in-flight pattern OR the candidate new pattern has `is_barrage=true`): the cross-pattern stagger floor relaxes to ≥ `BARRAGE_SIMULTANEITY_WINDOW_S` (PROVISIONAL 0.3s). The barrage carve-out authorizes near-simultaneous onsets within a designed barrage event; requiring full `TELEGRAPH_WINDOW_FLOOR_S` separation between a barrage pattern and any concurrent pattern would collapse the barrage to near-sequential and defeat Phase Identity.
  - **Last-onset anchor for barrages (R5 re-review 5 fix — closes level-designer BLOCKING B-1 4-onset envelope violation)**: for a barrage pattern in flight or admitted in the current spawn-decision cycle, the onset timestamp used in cross-pattern stagger computation is the **last (latest) scheduled onset** within the barrage window W. New non-barrage patterns are admissible no earlier than `last_barrage_onset + BARRAGE_SIMULTANEITY_WINDOW_S` (= last + 0.3s); new barrage-involved patterns are admissible no earlier than `last_barrage_onset + BARRAGE_SIMULTANEITY_WINDOW_S` (same — both cases relax to W per the carve-out). **Why last not first**: anchoring on the first onset (the prior R5 Cluster A draft) allowed a new pattern's onset to land at first + 0.3s = exactly when the barrage's third (M=3) onset fires, producing 4 telegraph onsets within a 0.3s window — exceeding the M=3 carve-out ceiling that bounds Pillar 2 survivability. Anchoring on the last scheduled onset preserves the M=3 envelope structurally: by the time any new pattern is admissible, all M barrage onsets have already fired. **Wave Spawner contract addition (small)**: the spawner must know the full barrage timeline (all M scheduled onset offsets relative to barrage start) at admission time. This is already required by the atomic pre-commitment mechanism (line 574); the last-onset anchor adds no new data requirement. **Trade-off vs first-onset anchor**: non-barrage density immediately after a barrage is reduced by an extra `(W - 0s) ≈ 0.3s` window. This is acceptable — the post-barrage interval is the natural "recovery breath" before the staggered cadence resumes, consistent with "the storm commits" → recovery pacing rhythm.
  - **Enforcement binding**: Wave Spawner MUST implement this check before issuing any new pattern start. A pattern start that would violate the applicable stagger floor is **deferred** to the next spawn decision cycle (not skipped permanently) — deferral preserves spawn density and avoids creating unintended "holes" in PEAK. If deferral would itself violate `max_concurrent_waves`, the deferred pattern is cancelled and a non-barrage replacement is drawn from the pool. Wave Spawner GDD must specify the deferral + replacement mechanism in its own rules.

- **max_concurrent_waves enforcement semantic (binding, R5 Cluster B Item 4 + R5 re-review 5 priority fix)**: The Wave Spawner's spawn-decision loop enforces the `max_concurrent_waves` cap at **pattern-start admission** granularity, not at per-wave-onset granularity. When Wave Spawner's spawn-decision fires, it computes `available_slots = max_concurrent_waves − currently_in_flight_waves` and compares against `required_slots` for the candidate pattern (1 for non-barrage; `MAX_PULLS_PER_BARRAGE` for barrage patterns). If `available_slots >= required_slots`, the pattern is admitted and all `required_slots` are pre-committed immediately (all M barrage onsets are counted as in-flight from the moment of admission, before individual onsets fire within W). If `available_slots < required_slots`, the pattern is **deferred** to the next spawn-decision cycle (deferral-not-skip, consistent with cross-pattern stagger policy item (12)). The deferred queue is bounded: at most one deferred pattern at a time.

  **Drop rule with `is_barrage` priority preservation (R5 re-review 5 fix + R6 timing/shadow-list clarification — closes systems-designer BLOCKING B-3 + level-designer BLOCKING B-2)**: If a second deferral candidate arrives while a pattern is already deferred, the priority preservation rule fires. **Replacement-draw timing (R6 binding)**: all "draw a non-barrage replacement" actions below draw from the **non-barrage shadow list** (NOT from the shuffle-bag cursor — see Shadow-list replacement mechanism above), and the replacement enters the **current spawn-decision cycle** if `available_slots ≥ 1`; otherwise the replacement is itself deferred (replacing the prior deferred-queue occupant if it was non-barrage, or simply dropped if a barrage is deferred — preserving the barrage). This eliminates the prior R5 ambiguity where "draw a replacement" did not specify cycle timing and could be interpreted as either current-cycle-admit (refilling slots and indefinitely deferring barrage) or next-cycle-queue (semantically unclear).
  - If the incoming candidate is `is_barrage=true` AND the deferred candidate is non-barrage: **evict the deferred non-barrage candidate from the queue, draw a non-barrage replacement from the shadow list to take its scheduling slot (admit in current cycle if `available_slots ≥ 1`; otherwise drop), and defer the incoming barrage candidate.** The barrage retains its position in the spawn sequence; the displaced non-barrage is replaced by a shadow-list draw (no shuffle-bag invariant broken — the displaced candidate was an interchangeable non-barrage and the shadow list bears no cross-run distribution obligation).
  - If the incoming candidate is non-barrage AND the deferred candidate is `is_barrage=true`: drop the incoming non-barrage, draw a non-barrage replacement from the shadow list for the spawn slot (admit in current cycle if `available_slots ≥ 1`). The deferred barrage stays deferred.
  - If both are non-barrage: drop the incoming non-barrage, draw a non-barrage replacement from the shadow list (admit in current cycle if `available_slots ≥ 1`).
  - If both are `is_barrage=true`: the second barrage is dropped and re-seeded into the shuffle-bag for a later spawn cycle (do NOT replace with a non-barrage — that would break the N≥1 structural guarantee). The shuffle-bag's reserved barrage slot must be re-issued in a subsequent spawn-decision cycle. **N≤3 ceiling check applies**: if `peak_barrage_count == BARRAGE_EVENTS_PER_PEAK_MAX (3)` when the re-seeded barrage is later redrawn, the redraw is converted to a non-barrage shadow-list replacement (per the N≤3 enforcement mechanism above) — this is the only path that intentionally drops a reserved-slot barrage, justified by the ceiling enforcement.

  **Why priority preservation is required**: without it, the shuffle-bag's structurally reserved barrage slot (per pattern selection rule constraint (3)) could be dropped by the prior drop rule and replaced with a non-barrage, producing N=0 barrages per PEAK on the unlucky timing — silently breaking the Phase Identity guarantee and the AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE precondition. The systems-designer R5 re-review 5 BLOCKING B-2 walkthrough confirmed this is reachable under the prior rule. With priority preservation, barrage candidates retain queue priority over non-barrage candidates at all admission decision points.

  **This semantic is required for barrage atomicity**: per-wave-onset enforcement would allow partial barrage admission (e.g., 1 of 3 onsets admitted then the cap fills), which silently truncates barrages, causes AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE to fail, and requires M to be revisited before Alpha. Pattern-start admission eliminates this failure mode by design. **Worst-case walkthrough** (PEAK, cap=3, barrage candidate needs M=3 slots): with 2 non-barrage waves in flight and 1 slot free, barrage candidate is deferred. After both in-flight waves drain, on the next spawn-decision cycle with 3 slots free, the barrage admits atomically — all 3 onsets pre-committed, all fire within W ≤ 0.3s as authored. Maximum deferral latency is bounded by natural drain (~1–2 spawn cycles at PEAK `wave_spawn_interval_s = 1.0s`); a brief density gap before a barrage onset is intentionally coherent with Phase Identity ("the storm commits").

**Knob interactions:**

- **`OPENER_END_NORMALIZED` and `PEAK_START_NORMALIZED` together** define the three phase widths. Their *difference* is MID's width — a narrow MID (e.g., `0.45` → `0.55`) produces a run that feels binary-OPENER-or-PEAK and should be avoided. Recommend MID width ≥ `0.25`.
- **`WaveSpawnIntervalCurve` and `MaxConcurrentWavesCurve` together** determine wave density. A short spawn interval × high concurrent cap can saturate the screen even if telegraph window is generous — tune them as a pair. Use playtest, not theory, for final values.
- **`TelegraphWindowCurve`** is the player's reaction budget. A curve that drops too fast in MID (e.g., from `0.94s` to `0.70s` over 5 seconds — R11a-arithmetic 2026-06-18 illustrative numbers under R10d-bound FLOOR=0.70s + Path B reauthoring; pre-R10d analogous example was `0.9s` to `0.65s`) feels jarring; a smooth ease-down is the design intent. ## Visual/Audio Requirements

DPC owns no visuals or audio directly. All phase-keyed feedback — transition stings, mix layer crossfades, particle bursts at phase change, music tempo shifts — is owned by downstream systems that subscribe to `current_phase` via snapshot diff. Same pattern as the Run State Machine's Visual/Audio section.

**Downstream contracts this GDD relies on** (each downstream GDD must specify implementation; this GDD names them so coverage gaps are visible):

- **Audio Controller** — On `previous_snapshot.current_phase != current_snapshot.current_phase`, fires the phase-transition sting and crossfades the music mix layer **only after applying Rule 16's `is_active` gate**. Per EC-7, the first-call contract on `GetPreviousFrameState()` prevents spurious stings at construction and hot-reload ONLY; Rule 16's gate prevents spurious stings on pause/resume (EC-9, EC-10); Rule 17's consumer-side init protocol prevents spurious stings on mid-run subscription. See Dependencies → Audio Controller row for the full 7-item binding contract: (1) sting category, (2) two acoustically distinct stings (creative identity = Audio Controller's domain), (3) ≤500ms crossfade with rationale tied to drain window, (4) sting/crossfade envelope coordination, (5) mix-resume onset on `is_active` rising edge, (6) per-phase acoustic differentiation requirement (testable, not "breathing over metronomic"), (7) short-`RUN_DURATION_S` sting density behavior.
- **Wave Spawner** — On phase transition, swaps the active pattern pool atomically.
- **Telegraph System** — Telegraph window scales smoothly with `telegraph_window_s` (continuous evaluation, no pop at phase boundary per Section D continuity invariant).
- **Pull-Wave Behavior** *(if it scales by phase)* — Pull velocity or behavior shift on phase change. Implementation TBD in Pull-Wave GDD.

No phase-transition visual is owned by DPC. If a "phase change" world-cue is desired beyond what downstream systems already produce (e.g., a global color shift at OPENER→MID), it belongs to a presentation system (Camera, Audio, VFX) and must subscribe to the phase diff — not be authored inside DPC.

## UI Requirements

DPC has no UI surface — no HUD widget, no phase indicator, no settings page exposed to the player. The In-Run HUD must not display the current phase, a phase progress bar, or any reified "you are now in PEAK" UI element. This is a Pillar 4 ("Surreal in Motion, Not in Menus") binding: the player feels the phase through the world's behavior — wave density, telegraph tightness, audio mix — not through UI chrome.

**Forbidden HUD elements** (recorded here so In-Run HUD GDD inherits the prohibition):

- Current-phase text label (e.g., "Phase 2 of 3", "Peak")
- Phase progress bar (segmented or continuous)
- Difficulty difficulty-tier icons or stars
- A run-completion timer visualization that exposes phase boundaries

The 60-second run timer itself (displayed by In-Run HUD) is owned by RSM, not DPC, and is exempt from this prohibition — RSM Rule 17 and Pillar 5 ("Skill Is Visible") require timer clarity in the final 1–2 seconds for wire-death attributability.

    **GIVEN** both OPENER→MID and MID→PEAK phase-transition stings are authored, the audio playback fixture is operational on the minimum-spec mobile device, and ≥ 30 blind testers each complete ≥ 10 A/B/X trials, **WHEN** tester responses are collected, **THEN** the AC is evaluated using a **per-tester primary metric (R5 re-review 5 fix — closes qa-lead BLOCKING B-2 and audio-director R-2 independence-violation findings)**:

    **Primary metric (per-tester pass/fail)**: Each tester's individual discrimination rate is computed as `count(this tester's correct X identifications) / count(this tester's total trials)`. A tester PASSES if their individual rate is ≥ 8 of 10 trials correct (effective threshold 80% at minimum 10-trial floor — note R6 correction: with integer trial counts at n=10, a "75% bar" requires 7.5/10 and cannot exist; the effective pass bar is 8/10 = 80%, kept explicit here to prevent reviewer arithmetic confusion). The AC passes if **≥ 23 of 30 testers individually pass** (≥ 77% of testers at the n=30 minimum).

    **Statistical analysis (R6 BLOCKING fix — qa-lead BLOCKING 1)**: applied at the per-tester level (n=30 independent subjects, k=23 passing). Under the null hypothesis H₀ "stings are indistinguishable," each tester's per-trial correct-ID rate is 0.5 (A/B/X chance). The probability a single tester individually scores ≥ 8/10 under H₀ is `p_null = P(X ≥ 8 | n=10, p=0.5) = (C(10,8) + C(10,9) + C(10,10)) / 2^10 = 56/1024 ≈ 0.0547`. The correct binomial null for the per-tester PASS proportion is therefore **p_null ≈ 0.055, NOT p_null = 0.5**. Under p_null = 0.055, k = 23 of n = 30 testers passing yields a one-sided binomial p-value far below 0.001 (effectively zero) — overwhelming evidence against chance. The prior R5 statement "p ≈ 0.002 against null 50%" used the wrong null and is hereby corrected; the directional conclusion (strong evidence against chance) is unchanged but stronger than R5 reported.

    **Why per-tester (not aggregate 300-trial)**: aggregating 30 × 10 = 300 trials as independent binomial samples is statistically invalid because within-tester trials are correlated (each tester forms a perceptual model across their 10 trials; learning effects bias responses after the first few exposures). The aggregate-trial Wilson LCB at n=300, k=225 (75%) ≈ 0.70 is illusory when effective independent N ≈ 30. The per-tester metric uses subjects as the unit of analysis, where independence is satisfied.

    **Secondary metric (aggregate, reported but not gate)**: aggregate trial pass rate `count(all correct identifications) / count(all trials)` is reported alongside the per-tester pass count for diagnostic context only. A divergence between per-tester pass rate and aggregate trial rate (e.g., aggregate passes 80% but per-tester fails) indicates a within-tester learning artifact — treat as Path B (per-sting fail, see Resolution Paths below).

    **Statistical interpretation**: the 8/10 per-tester threshold and ≥77% subjects-passing requirement together produce a robust binomial signal against the corrected null p_null ≈ 0.055. The 8/10 per-tester threshold is consistent with the established ≥75%-ish A/B/X discrimination criterion in blind listening test literature (R6 citation fix — audio-director R6-2 BLOCKING): the correct references are Burstein (1988, "Approximation Formulas for Error Risk and Sample Size in ABX Testing," J. Audio Eng. Soc.) and Boley & Lester (2009, "Statistical Analysis of ABX Results Using Signal Detection Theory," AES Convention 127). The prior R5 citation "ITU-R BS.1116-3" was wrong — that standard is the hidden-reference triple-stimulus test for codec impairment evaluation using a 5-point CCIR continuous quality scale with Student's t statistics, which defines no 75% binomial threshold and is not the methodology used here. The per-tester threshold is deliberately lower than AC-PILLAR-2-BARRAGE's 80% gameplay-survival threshold because sting discrimination is a purely perceptual listening task (no reaction-time stress, no physical input required).

    **Resolution paths**:
      - **Path A (pass)**: discrimination rate ≥ 75% AND p < 0.05. Both stings are locked as authored. Audio Controller GDD's item 2 AC is closed. The sting pair is admitted to the final game build.
      - **Path B (fail — one sting indistinguishable)**: aggregate discrimination < 75%, AND per-sting confusion analysis isolates the failure to one sting. Resolution: re-design the failing sting (Audio Controller GDD content authoring fix); re-run AC on the revised pair with a NEW sample of n ≥ 30 testers (avoids test-retest familiarity contamination). The passing sting is locked.
      - **Path C (systemic fail — stings indistinguishable after ≥ 2 redesign cycles)**: discrimination consistently < 75% across iterations. Indicates the two-momentary-sting architecture is insufficient.
