## 1. Overview

Player Movement (Slip) — Presentation defines PM's player-facing perception surface: the visual flashes, audio cues, ducking + triple-overlap resolution, haptic dispatch, and the Performance-mode banner that translate mechanics-layer state transitions and platform-layer broadcasts into things the player can see, hear, or feel. This sub-GDD owns the perception contract for: the Commitment-Tell `LeadingFaceFlash` (visual peak ±0.80; 2-frame hold + 50 ms decay; 200 ms cooldown cadence cap per R11a-11); the Near-Miss Beat (avatar Y-dip + breath-like audio swell + opt-in `EHapticEvent::NearMiss` haptic gated on `near_miss_haptic_enabled` per R11a-12); the Hardware-Performance Banner (locked copy "Performance mode — hardest barrage suppressed." anchored below the OS safe-area top inset per R11a-9/R11a-10; trigger consumed from `player-movement-platform.md`'s watchdog broadcast); the Audio table (cue identities + center-pan locked decision per R11a-14 + `audio_cue_ratio` safe range [0.89, 1.12] per R11a-13); cue interaction policy (ducking under near-miss swell + triple-overlap resolution per R11a-15 + raised-cosine HARD-CUT ramp per R12a B-AUDIO-2 closure); and the Audio-Visual Ownership Split that defines which sub-GDD owns which perception signal. The mechanics layer (`player-movement-mechanics.md`) supplies the transition triggers; the platform layer (`player-movement-platform.md`) supplies the watchdog broadcast and the `IHapticDispatch::IsSystemHapticsEnabled()` OS-state gate. The HUD GDD (when authored) inherits the banner copy + safe-area binding + "barrage" player-facing vocabulary establishment as BLOCKING forward contracts. Speaker-mode mobile players cannot hear stereo pan — therefore all directional information must be readable from visual cues alone (pan-as-aesthetic is RETIRED per R11a-14).

## 2. Player Fantasy

For the player, every perception signal answers one question: *did the moment I felt land?* The slip cue is the sound of the body refusing to be pulled. The commitment-tell flash is the proof that the input was received before the body began the arc. The near-miss beat is the breath after the wave passes — the audible margin of the read that was almost too late. The Performance-mode banner is the honest disclosure that the device cannot keep the promise the game made, and the player deserves to know *which* promise is being relaxed.

**The presentation contract demands:**

- **Honest perception.** Every mechanics transition that materially changes state has a perception signal (Pillar 5). Buffer drops have a haptic. Commitments have a flash. Near-misses have a beat. Edge-absorbs have a damped cue. No state change is silent. (See §3 Detailed Rules — Audio-Visual Ownership Split for the full per-event ownership table.)
- **No over-signal.** Perception signals stay below the threshold of "you were hit." Soft haptics, low-luminance flashes (80 % white per R11a-11, not 100 %), audio cues that duck rather than dominate. The slip cue ends slightly before the tween settles — the cue is in service of the gesture, not the centerpiece of it.
- **No directional signal in pan.** Speaker-mode mobile players cannot hear stereo pan. All directional information must be visible. Pan is aesthetic only — slip cues are center-pan post-R11a-14; the pre-R11a panned slip variant is RETIRED.
- **Honest disclosure on degradation.** When the platform layer detects sustained sub-spec performance and broadcasts `OnHardwarePerformanceBreach(true)`, the banner discloses **which** difficulty class was suppressed (the M=3 PEAK barrage class). Players never accrue PBs against an invisibly-easier game. The banner copy is "Performance mode — hardest barrage suppressed." — non-alarming, comprehensible, class-disclosing.
- **Accessibility is a default-aware setting, not a downgrade.** The opt-in `near_miss_haptic_enabled` toggle exists because deaf-in-speaker-mode players have no other way to perceive the near-miss confirmation. The default state (off) preserves the design intent for sighted-hearing players; the toggle exists for the players who would otherwise be excluded. Same default-aware framing applies to the `commitment_tell_flash_enabled` / `reduce_motion` setting added at R12a to close B-CERT-1 (App Store accessibility cert).

## 3. Detailed Rules

This section defines what the player *perceives* during each PM event, and which system owns each perception signal. Visual signals are listed first; audio/haptic second. Pan direction is aesthetic-only — all directional information must be readable from visual cues alone (speaker-mode mobile players cannot hear stereo pan).

### Commitment-Tell (2-frame hold + 50 ms decay)

When PM mechanics layer transitions SETTLED→SLIPPING, before any translation arc begins and in the same frame as collision commitment:

- **Visual (R11a §5 DR-D.3 amended — peak luminance reduced 100% → 80%; closes IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT photosensitivity defect at SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 buffer-flush chains):** The leading-edge face of the avatar's torso voxel block (the face pointing toward `target_lane`) brightens to **80 % white** (NOT 100 % white — see R11a-11 closure), holds for a minimum of 2 frames (33 ms at 60 fps; 66 ms at 30 fps), then decays to normal over 50 ms via a material parameter fade (`LeadingFaceFlash` scalar parameter: +0.80 = right face, −0.80 = left face; PM mechanics sets to ±0.80 on transition, holds for 2 frames, then begins the 50 ms fade to 0.0). No geometry change. No scale change. Arc begins during the hold. Minimum visible duration: 33 ms under any supported frame rate. The pre-R11a ±1.0 peak is RETIRED — 100 % luminance contrast against the dark avatar exceeded the PEAT criterion at the cadence buffer-flush chains can produce; 80 % preserves perceptibility while clearing the PEAT bar at the worst-case cadence under R11a-11's cadence cap.
- **Trigger (R11a §5 DR-D.3 amended — ≤ 5 fires per second cadence cap; closes IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT photosensitivity defect at SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 buffer-flush chains):** Fires on every SETTLED→SLIPPING transition, including buffer-flush execution (buffer-flush is a SETTLED→SLIPPING transition — collision commits to `target_lane` in that frame), **subject to a 5-per-second visual cadence cap**: if less than **200 ms** has elapsed since the previous tell's `LeadingFaceFlash` faded to zero (i.e., between the prior tell's fade-end timestamp and the current SETTLED→SLIPPING transition timestamp), the `LeadingFaceFlash` flash is **suppressed for the current transition** (`LeadingFaceFlash` remains at 0.0 — no flash this transition). The SETTLED→SLIPPING transition itself proceeds normally — collision still commits, the tween starts, and `commitment_tell_fire_count` still increments. Only the visual flash is gated. Suppression is per-transition; subsequent transitions outside the 200 ms window resume normal flashes. Does NOT fire for edge-absorb inputs (PM stays SETTLED — no transition occurs). `commitment_tell_fire_count` increments by 1 each time the underlying transition fires, regardless of whether the visual flash was suppressed by the cadence cap — the counter reflects commitment, not flash render. At SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 (the worst-case buffer-flush chain cadence), the theoretical maximum SETTLED→SLIPPING transitions per second is ~10 (each tween + buffer-flush completes in ~100 ms); the 200 ms gating clamps the perceived flash cadence to ≤ 5/sec for the duration of any sustained chain.
- **Owned by:** Presentation owns the `LeadingFaceFlash` rendering + cadence cap. Mechanics (`player-movement-mechanics.md`) owns the SETTLED→SLIPPING transition that triggers it. Art must author the `LeadingFaceFlash` material parameter on the torso voxel's leading face mesh material. PM mechanics layer sets the parameter at runtime via a Material Instance Dynamic.
- **Design intent:** Satisfies Pillar 5 (Skill Is Visible) — the player sees their commitment register before the body moves. The flash answers "did my input land?" at the moment collision commits to the destination lane. A player who times their slip precisely sees the flash before the wave reaches their committed lane.

### Near-Miss Beat

When Pull-Wave detects a qualifying near-miss and calls `TriggerNearMissBeat()` on PM:

- **Visual:** Avatar dips 2–3 % on the Y axis (breath-inward motion) over 33 ms, returns over 80 ms. Maximum 5 % body displacement. **The dip is a procedural additive component-local Z-offset on the mesh (`MeshLocalOffset.Z` driven by a local animation timer), applied independently of and summed with F-5 lean rotation and F-3 lateral translation. The tween does NOT pause during the near-miss beat — F-2 continues advancing TweenProgress normally.** `lean_angle`, `head_lean_angle`, and `arm_lean_angle` are NOT modified by the near-miss beat. The Y-dip and the tween lean are independent additive contributions to the mesh transform.
- **Audio:** Breath-like mid-frequency swell (600 Hz – 1.6 kHz), 200–300 ms, centered (no pan), -6 dB relative to slip cue (see Audio cue table below). The audio intentionally outlasts the visual by up to 190 ms — the breath lingers. This is by design.
- **Haptic (R11a §5 DR-D.4 NEW — opt-in accessibility setting):** Default = **off** (preserves the current "no haptic over-signals" design intent for sighted hearing players). When the player enables the runtime accessibility setting **`near_miss_haptic_enabled = true`** (starting-draft settings copy: "Near-Miss Haptic Feedback — Adds a soft vibration when waves pass close." — neutral framing, no diagnostic-class user-labeling; final copy owned by accessibility-specialist at Polish gate, see AC-NEARMISS-HAPTIC), PM fires `IHapticDispatch::Fire(EHapticEvent::NearMiss)` synchronously with the visual Y-dip onset. `EHapticEvent::NearMiss` is a sub-50 ms low-amplitude soft pulse — distinct from `EHapticEvent::SlipConfirmed` (the SETTLED→SLIPPING commit tell on Input System's haptic vocabulary) and `EHapticEvent::BufferDrop` (the Rule 3 buffer-full sting; sharper / shorter). Soft = low-amplitude = below the conscious "you were hit" threshold; deliberately quieter than `SlipConfirmed`. Synchronization: haptic fires within ±50 ms of the visual Y-dip onset (matches the Audio haptic/audio synchronization tolerance). When the setting is `false` (the default), no haptic dispatch fires — current design intent preserved. The setting lives in HUD/Accessibility Settings GDD (when authored); PM's haptic dispatch is gated by reading `IGameSettings::IsNearMissHapticEnabled()` (or equivalent settings-bridge interface) inside `TriggerNearMissBeat()` before the dispatch — AND by the platform-layer OS-state gate `IHapticDispatch::IsSystemHapticsEnabled()` per R12a B-CERT-2 closure (iOS Focus / Android DND). Closes the deaf-in-speaker-mode accessibility gap (PM Player Fantasy acknowledges this player class; pre-R11a near-miss confirmation was inaudible-and-sub-perceptual-visual for them, no haptic alternative). Accessibility-specialist signs off on the setting default state + copy wording at Polish gate. Forward contracts: HUD/Accessibility Settings GDD inherits the `near_miss_haptic_enabled` toggle on first authoring; Input System GDD's haptic-vocabulary table (`input-system.md` §Haptic Vocabulary) inherits the new `EHapticEvent::NearMiss` event on first revision after R11a APPROVED.
- **Trigger API (R7-PM-PROPAGATION-REVIEW — stale OnSlipMidpoint detection-input language corrected):** Pull-Wave calls `TriggerNearMissBeat()` on PM after Pull-Wave Rule 11 detects a qualifying near-miss by reading `PM.current_lane` directly at the wave's LANDED-entry tick (PM Rules 4 + 7 — owned by `player-movement-mechanics.md` — guarantee source-lane semantic during SLIPPING; see mechanics §3 Public Interface). `OnSlipMidpoint` is NOT used as the detection input under R7+; the prior delegate-based detection path was superseded by Pull-Wave R1 RC-A 2026-06-07. Mechanics owns the source-lane invariant; presentation owns the animation playback. `TriggerNearMissBeat()` may be called during SETTLED or SLIPPING — the Y-dip composites additively with any in-flight tween lean.
- **Owned by:** Presentation owns the avatar animation response (dip + return) + audio swell + haptic dispatch. Mechanics owns the `TriggerNearMissBeat()` call site contract. Pull-Wave owns the trigger decision.

### Hardware-Performance Banner (R10a §1.3 + §5.3 (a) NEW — player-facing UX for sustained sub-60-fps degradation)

When PM's Hardware Contract runtime DT watchdog (owned by `player-movement-platform.md` § Hardware Contract) detects sustained sub-55 fps operation (rolling-window breach), the §5.3 (a) Banner Notification is the player-facing UX:

- **Visual:** A thin horizontal banner reading **"Performance mode — hardest barrage suppressed."** (R11a §5 DR-D.1 NEW — supersedes pre-R11a dev-jargon copy "Performance reduced — survivability adjustments active." which failed the R11a §6.3 disclosure contract; see R11a-9 header decision for full closure). Banner is `~24 px` tall on a 6-inch portrait mobile screen. Banner color uses an attention-orange tone (specific hex / palette assignment is owned by the HUD GDD + UX-designer; placeholder: `#F4A04A` on `#1A1A1A` background; readable but not alarming). Banner fades in over 250 ms from 0 % to 100 % opacity on watchdog breach entry; fades out over 250 ms on watchdog hysteresis-release.
- **Placement (R11a §5 DR-D.2 NEW — closes silent-occlusion defect on notched iOS / Android status-bar devices):** Banner is anchored **below the OS safe-area top inset**, NOT at the screen-edge `y=0`. On iOS, the banner's top edge sits at `safeAreaInsets.top` (30–59 pt on iPhone X+ devices with notch / Dynamic Island; 20 pt on home-button devices). On Android, the banner's top edge sits at the bottom of `WindowInsetsCompat.Type.systemBars()` (24–28 dp on devices with a status bar). The banner extends downward into the HUD overlay area for its ~24 px / dp height. Rendering at `y=0` is FORBIDDEN — that position is silently occluded on 60 %+ of the iOS fleet (iPhone X 2017+) and any Android with a status bar visible. HUD GDD inherits this safe-area binding as a BLOCKING layout constraint on first authoring (forward contract — see Cross-System Interface Table HUD row in `player-movement-platform.md`).
- **Audio:** None. The banner is silent — performance degradation is visual-only because audio cues might mask gameplay-critical near-miss / slip cues. UX-designer + audio-director co-sign on the silent default.
- **Haptic:** None. Same rationale as audio.
- **Trigger:** Platform layer's watchdog fires `BroadcastHardwarePerformanceBreach(true)` when `bSustainedSub55 || bHitchCluster` is detected (see `player-movement-platform.md` § Hardware Contract → DT watchdog spec). HUD subscribes to the broadcast and renders the banner.
- **Release:** Platform layer fires `BroadcastHardwarePerformanceBreach(false)` after the hysteresis-release condition (60 clean samples × 3.0 s sustained). HUD fades the banner.
- **Owned by:** Platform owns the watchdog trigger/release (`is_hw_performance_degraded` public property + broadcast — see `player-movement-platform.md` §3 Public Interface). HUD owns the visual banner rendering. Presentation (this sub-GDD) owns the banner copy + visual styling + safe-area binding + disclosure contract. UX-designer owns the per-platform visual styling.
- **Design intent:** Per-run honesty for Pillar 5. Players on devices below SLIPSTORM's min-spec receive a player-visible acknowledgment that the game has shifted into the M=2 PEAK barrage suppression mode (per platform layer's §5.2 (a) survivability margin relaxation forward contract on Wave Spawner). The Pillar-5-honest path is: tell the player their device is degraded, do NOT silently change the difficulty. The banner is the "silently change" trap-door.
- **Disclosure contract (R11a §6.3 — closes DR-F.3 two-class-skill defect; CLOSED by R11a §5 DR-D.1 = R11a-9 banner copy):** The banner copy MUST disclose **what** difficulty class is being suppressed, not merely that "adjustments are active." Without the disclosure, players on degraded devices accrue PBs against an easier difficulty class (the §5.2 (a) M=3 PEAK gate suppresses the hardest barrage tier; PB calibration against M=2-only is materially easier than full-difficulty PB) and never know they're playing a different game. Pillar 5 ("Skill Is Visible") fails by invisibility: the player has an invisible skill ceiling. The disclosure contract requires the copy to surface the suppressed class explicitly. **The contract is closed by R11a-9 = "Performance mode — hardest barrage suppressed."** which is intended to satisfy all three constraints: (1) **player-comprehensible** — *contingent on the HUD player-facing-vocabulary forward contract below*; (2) **non-alarming tone** (no "device below spec", no error-color iconography — orange ≠ red); (3) **discloses suppressed class** (the player learns that M=3 PEAK is gated in this run, which is the Pillar-5 disclosure the contract demands). **Contingent vocabulary clause (R11a §5 DR-D.1 — flagged for Polish-gate verification)**: as of R11a authoring, "barrage" appears ONLY in design-system docs (`pull-wave-behavior.md` / `difficulty-phase-controller.md` / `entities.yaml`), NOT in any player-facing string. The HUD GDD (unauthored at R11a) inherits a BLOCKING forward contract to establish "barrage" as player-facing vocabulary — via the wave-class readout, death-replay summary screen, or tutorial — **before** the banner copy ships. If HUD authoring elects different player-facing terminology (e.g., "wave class 3" or a glyph-only readout), DR-D.1 must be re-opened and the banner copy revised in lockstep. The R11a §6.3 + §5 DR-D.1 coupling is fulfilled at the **GDD level**; final player-facing readability is a Polish-gate co-sign (UX-designer + creative-director verify the HUD vocabulary contract was honored before approving the banner).

**Failure mode the banner DOES NOT cover**: a player who never sees the banner because their device's framerate is steady-but-low (e.g., sustained 50 fps without any sub-55 dips would NOT trigger the watchdog under R10a §1.3 thresholds; sustained 30 fps WOULD trigger). The watchdog thresholds are tuned to the §5.2 (a) gate's actual breach point — devices that hold 55 fps + are not degraded enough to warrant the gate, but also not 60 fps. Polish-phase performance-analyst confirms whether to add a secondary banner for "marginal performance" at 55–60 fps.

### Visual/Audio Requirements

#### Slip Tween Animation

The slip is a lean, not a translation. At 150ms default (100–150ms tunable per `player-movement-mechanics.md` §7; all timing ratios below scale proportionally):

- **Phase 1 — Lean-in (0–30ms):** Body pivots toward the destination via actor local X axis rotation (`SetRelativeRotation` on mesh component — not actor root; see `player-movement-mechanics.md` §3 Cross-Component Interfaces > Rotation Implementation). No translation yet. `LEAN_CURVE_ASSET` (mechanics §4 F-5) drives the rotation from 0 to `MAX_LEAN_ANGLE_DEG`. Leading arm/shoulder (`arm_lean_angle`) leads by ~8ms (`ARM_LEAD_PROGRESS` = 0.05, reads LeanCurve ahead of body). Head (`head_lean_angle`) lags by ~16ms (`HEAD_LAG_PROGRESS` = 0.10, reads LeanCurve behind body).
- **Phase 2 — Cross (30–120ms):** Lateral translation on the `SLIP_CURVE_ASSET` arc (mechanics §4 F-3). Body holds lean angle throughout. No upright correction until Phase 3.
- **Phase 3 — Settle (120–150ms):** Translation completes (world-position overshoot prevented by mechanics §4 F-3 clamp). Body rotation returns to neutral via `LEAN_CURVE_ASSET` — rotation dips briefly past neutral (~16ms reverse lean, authored into curve between t=0.90–1.0) before settling to 0. Head catches up at t=0.90 (neutral, no snap).

**Phase 1 shelf:** Phase 1's zero-translation hold is implemented by the `SLIP_CURVE_ASSET` Phase 1 shelf (f = 0 for TweenProgress [0.0, 0.20]) — not by a separate animation blend. `SLIP_CURVE_ASSET` drives `lateral_world_position` (mechanics §4 F-3); `LEAN_CURVE_ASSET` drives body rotation (mechanics §4 F-5). These are two independent curves and must be synchronized: the body pivot (F-5 lean) and zero translation (F-3 shelf) must occupy the same TweenProgress window.

**Voxel implementation:** No skin deformation. "Bend" is achieved through actor local X axis rotation (8–12° hip pivot driven by `LEAN_CURVE_ASSET`), non-uniform voxel scaling on the leading side (5–8% X compression on torso block at peak lean), and delayed child-node rotation for head and arm blocks (`HEAD_LAG_PROGRESS` = 0.10 phase offset).

**Edge-absorb mash policy:** If the player mashes slip-left repeatedly while SETTLED at FarLeft, each input triggers the edge-absorb animation. Each new slip input while the edge-absorb animation is still playing restarts it from the beginning (interrupt and restart — not queue).

**Do NOT produce:** translation with no body tilt; tilt that starts after translation begins; vertical bounce arc; motion-blur trails; squash on destination arrival.

#### Commitment-Tell Visual (R11a §5 DR-D.3 — peak ±1.0 → ±0.80; ≤ 5 fires/sec cadence cap)

On SETTLED→SLIPPING transition: the leading-edge face of the torso voxel block brightens to **80 % white** (`LeadingFaceFlash` scalar parameter set to ±0.80 at transition onset; pre-R11a ±1.0 RETIRED for PEAT compliance — see R11a-11 + § Commitment-Tell above), holds for a minimum of 2 frames (33 ms at 60 fps; 66 ms at 30 fps), then decays to normal over 50 ms via a material parameter fade (PM fades to 0.0 over the 50 ms tail). The flash is **suppressed entirely if less than 200 ms has elapsed since the prior tell's fade-to-zero moment** — the cadence cap at SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 buffer-flush chains; see § Commitment-Tell above for the full semantics. Not the whole avatar — leading face only. No geometry or scale change.

#### Edge-Absorb Animation

When the player inputs toward the track edge from the leftmost or rightmost lane, the avatar performs an attempted lean that the world does not complete:

- Lean-in to 30–40% of full lean amplitude (~3–4° hip pivot) — identical to slip Phase 1.
- Hold for 40ms with micro-tremor (16–33ms oscillation, <5% of lean angle). The body presses against an invisible wall.
- Damped return over ~200ms with slow ease-out (visibly slower than slip settle — effort withdrawn, not snapped). Total duration ~270ms.

The lean-in must reach at least 30% of full slip lean amplitude to be readable at mobile screen sizes.

Timing math is owned by `player-movement-mechanics.md` §4 F-6; presentation owns the visual envelope above (lean amplitude proportions + micro-tremor parameters + damped-return read).

**Do NOT produce:** lean that fully completes then snaps back; instantaneous return to neutral; any camera or screen response.

#### Near-Miss Beat Visual (R11a §5 DR-D.4 — opt-in haptic)

When Pull-Wave calls `TriggerNearMissBeat()` on PM (mechanics forwards to presentation per cross-sub-GDD forward contract): avatar dips 2–3% on the Y axis (breath-inward, not a crouch) over ~0ms (instantaneous onset), Y-dip holds at peak Z-offset for 33ms, then returns over 80ms. No additional lateral translation from this beat — any active tween continues advancing via mechanics §4 F-2 normally. No modification to `lean_angle`, `head_lean_angle`, or `arm_lean_angle`. Maximum 5% body displacement.

**Haptic (R11a §5 DR-D.4 — opt-in accessibility setting):** When the player has enabled `near_miss_haptic_enabled` (off by default), PM dispatches `EHapticEvent::NearMiss` — sub-50 ms low-amplitude soft pulse — synchronously with the visual Y-dip onset (within ±50 ms). When the setting is off (default), no haptic fires. See § Near-Miss Beat above for the full setting copy + design rationale. Closes the deaf-in-speaker-mode accessibility gap. Haptic OS-state gating (iOS Focus / Android DND) is owned by `player-movement-platform.md` § Public Interface (Platform-Side) per B-CERT-2.

**Do NOT produce:** camera shake; screen flash, vignette pulse, or chromatic aberration; slowdown or hit-stop; a tilt toward the wave direction (reads as being hit); any reaction >5% body displacement; **haptic dispatch when `near_miss_haptic_enabled == false`** (the off-default is load-bearing for the current sighted-hearing design intent — over-firing the haptic regardless of setting state would defeat the opt-in contract).

#### DEAD Freeze

Translation halts at the exact fractional tween position on the death frame. `lean_angle`, `head_lean_angle`, and `arm_lean_angle` all hold their mid-arc values — no ease-out, no settle, no return to neutral. A 16ms 10% scale-down pulse at the freeze frame is acceptable as punctuation (no ease-in, no shake).

Mechanics drives the freeze (`player-movement-mechanics.md` §3 Rule 7 + §4 F-5/F-6 freeze semantics); presentation owns the optional scale-down pulse + the "no ease-out / no ragdoll / no return-to-lane" visual contract.

**Do NOT produce:** ease-out that completes the translation post-death; ragdoll or impact reaction; return-to-lane animation before the death screen; camera shake.

#### COMPLETE / ABORTED Snap

**COMPLETE:** PM snaps `lateral_world_position` to `lane_world_x(target_lane)` and `lean_angle` / `head_lean_angle` / `arm_lean_angle` to 0° in the same frame as the COMPLETE state transition, regardless of `TweenProgress`. Hard position-and-rotation reset — no Phase 3 settle, no partial arc, no ease-out. No additional landing emphasis.

**ABORTED:** Identical snap behavior: `lateral_world_position = lane_world_x(target_lane)`, `lean_angle = head_lean_angle = arm_lean_angle = 0°`, `TweenProgress = 0.0`. Tween arc does not complete.

Mechanics drives the snap (`player-movement-mechanics.md` §3 Rules 8/9 + §4 F-5 reset semantics); presentation owns the "no additional landing emphasis" visual contract.

#### Audio

> **Locked Decision — Pan-as-aesthetic is RETIRED (R11a §4 DR-C.4 = R11a-14).** All slip cues are **center-pan (no pan)**. The pre-R11a "pan follows slip direction" decision is closed. Rationale: 30–40 % of mobile players use headphones; for them, the pre-R11a panned slip cue + mono-front edge-absorb cue created an implicit "pan-absence = edge-absorb / pan-present = slip" directional signal, which violates the Pillar-2 read-from-visuals-only constraint (a directional signal implicitly encoded into pan vs absence-of-pan is still a directional signal — speaker-mode players hear neither). The R10a §2.10 / §5.5 "center-pan slip variant" carve-out (interleave-or-retire-at-polish open contract) is now CLOSED on the retire path: there is one slip cue, it is center-pan, no panned variant exists. Edge-absorb audio continues mono-front unchanged. All directional information remains readable from visual cues (body lean, commitment-tell) alone — this is unchanged.

All cues: primary band 300Hz–2kHz, -14 LUFS mobile mix, mono-safe (no phase cancellation in fold-down).

**Overlapping cues — priority hierarchy (highest wins; R10a §2.9 + §5.4 NEW carve-out for near-miss; R11a §4 C.3 = R11a-15 NEW resolution table for triple-overlap):** buffer-drop > slip > edge-absorb. If two of those cues would play simultaneously, the higher-priority cue plays at full volume; the lower-priority cue is suppressed for its duration. If two slips chain immediately (buffer fires on tween completion), the second slip cue replaces the first — no ducking, no overlap. **Near-miss is a special case (R10a §2.9 / §5.4 — supersedes prior R7+ "slip > near-miss" suppression which broke Pillar 5 for slip+near-miss-in-same-beat)**: when a near-miss swell would play simultaneously with a slip cue (Pull-Wave Rule 11 fires `TriggerNearMissBeat()` during an active slip cue), the slip cue is **ducked by `-6 dB` with a 50 ms attack and 100 ms release** for the duration of the near-miss swell; both cues play. The near-miss swell is NOT ducked under any of the four PM-owned cues — it is the rarest event and must always read clearly. Near-miss is also not ducked under buffer-drop (the player who buffer-drops during a near-miss has already informed the system — surfacing the near-miss read is still Pillar-5-load-bearing).

**Triple-overlap resolution table (R11a §4 C.3 = R11a-15 NEW — closes pre-R11a undefined-state defect when buffer-drop + slip + near-miss are simultaneously active; bilateral priority hierarchy alone produces contradictory state on the slip cue: simultaneously suppressed by buffer-drop priority AND ducked by near-miss carve-out):**

| Active cues | Resolution |
|---|---|
| buffer-drop only | buffer-drop plays at full |
| slip only | slip plays at full |
| near-miss only | near-miss plays at full |
| buffer-drop + slip | buffer-drop plays at full; slip is HARD-CUT at suppression onset (≤ 5 ms ramp to preserve buffer-drop click clarity — abrupt enough to read as "buffer-drop wins", short enough to avoid a click artefact in the cut) |
| buffer-drop + near-miss | both play at full (near-miss is never ducked under any PM cue; buffer-drop priority hierarchy does NOT apply to near-miss) |
| slip + near-miss | slip ducked −6 dB / 50 ms attack / 100 ms release; near-miss plays at full (R10a §2.9 + §5.4 carve-out, clarified by R11a §4 C.2 vacuous-case rule above) |
| **buffer-drop + slip + near-miss** (triple) | **buffer-drop plays at full; slip is HARD-CUT (buffer-drop priority wins over the slip-vs-near-miss ducking carve-out); near-miss plays at full. Net audible: buffer-drop + near-miss only.** Rationale: the buffer-drop suppression of the slip cue (5 ms ramp) dominates the near-miss ducking of the slip cue (50 ms attack to −6 dB); resolving the slip cue toward HARD-CUT eliminates the contradictory "ducked-AND-suppressed" state. The near-miss read remains audible (it is never ducked under any PM cue), so Pillar-5 near-miss surfacing is preserved. The buffer-drop click reads clean against the near-miss swell because the swell is a low-frequency breath (600 Hz–1.6 kHz) and the buffer-drop click is a 300–600 Hz dry percussive — spectrally separable enough to read independently. |

**Haptic / audio synchronization tolerance:** Haptic and audio events that are designed to fire simultaneously (e.g., buffer-drop haptic + buffer-drop sting) must fire within ±50 ms of each other. Larger offsets decouple the proprioceptive and auditory signals and undermine the "registered" feel.

## 4. Formulas

Presentation owns three perception-scoped formula contracts: audio cue duration anchoring (slip-cue identity guarantee), commitment-tell cadence-cap timing (PEAT-bound flash rate ceiling), and the triple-overlap HARD-CUT gain ramp envelope. Mechanical formulas that *drive* what the player perceives (F-2 SLIP timing, F-3 lateral position, F-5 lean output, F-6 EC-15 fade-out) live in `player-movement-mechanics.md` §4 — presentation consumes their outputs and does not redefine them.

### F-AUDIO-CUE-DURATION — Slip cue duration formal contract (R10a §2.8 promoted; R11a §4 DR-C.1 = R11a-13 safe-range tightening)

The slip cue's runtime duration is a derived value anchored to the mechanics-owned SLIP_TWEEN duration via the presentation-owned `audio_cue_ratio` knob (§7):

```
audio_cue_duration_s = SLIP_TWEEN_DURATION_S × audio_cue_ratio
```

**Variables**:
- `SLIP_TWEEN_DURATION_S` — owned by `player-movement-mechanics.md` §7. Default 0.15 s; safe range [0.10, 0.15] s.
- `audio_cue_ratio` — owned by §7 below. Default **0.93** (R11a-13); safe range **[0.89, 1.12]** (R11a-13 tightened from R10a's [0.70, 1.00]).
- `audio_cue_duration_s` — derived. Bounded by the joint safe-range corners: minimum 89 ms (0.10 × 0.89); maximum 168 ms (0.15 × 1.12); default tuning 139.5 ms (0.15 × 0.93).

**Example calculations**:
- Default tuning: `0.15 × 0.93 = 139.5 ms` — cue ends 10.5 ms before tween settles, by design ("this is happening" not "this happened").
- SLIP_TWEEN safe-floor + default ratio: `0.10 × 0.93 = 93 ms` — minimum cue duration across joint safe range; still inside the readable-whoosh 85–150 ms band documented in §3 Audio > Slip cue row.
- audio_cue_ratio safe-ceiling at default SLIP_TWEEN: `0.15 × 1.12 = 168 ms` — cue overhangs tween by 18 ms (~10 %); design-accepted modest overhang.

### F-AUDIO-CUE-IDENTITY — Safe-range derivation against ±2-semitone identity bound (R11a §4 DR-C.1 = R11a-13)

The `audio_cue_ratio` safe range [0.89, 1.12] is derived from the "same cue" perceptual threshold for percussive whoosh content: ±2 semitones of pitch shift via playback-rate transposition.

Assuming the master audio asset is authored at the default cue duration (139.5 ms at the default tuning), runtime playback-rate scaling at the safe-range bounds gives:

```
rate_min = SLIP_TWEEN_default / audio_cue_duration_max = 0.15 / 0.168 ≈ 0.893
rate_max = SLIP_TWEEN_default / audio_cue_duration_min = 0.15 / 0.1335 ≈ 1.124
```

The pitch shift in semitones for a rate ratio `r` is `12 × log₂(r)`:

```
semitone_shift_min = 12 × log₂(0.893) ≈ −1.96 semitones
semitone_shift_max = 12 × log₂(1.124) ≈ +2.02 semitones
```

Both endpoints fall within ±2 semitones (±0.165 octaves) — the established "same cue" perceptual threshold for percussive whoosh content. The slip whoosh therefore stays inside the 400–1200 Hz spectral band at all safe-range tunings.

**Why the prior [0.70, 1.00] safe range was rejected**: at `rate = 1/0.70 ≈ 1.43×`, the pitch shift is `12 × log₂(1.43) ≈ +6.18 semitones`, transposing the 400–1200 Hz slip whoosh into the 600–1600 Hz near-miss swell spectral band — cue identity destroyed; the slip becomes confusable with the near-miss beat. The R11a-13 tightening to [0.89, 1.12] closes this perceptual collision path.

### F-COMMIT-CADENCE-CAP — Visual flash rate ceiling timing (R11a §5 DR-D.3 = R11a-11)

The Commitment-Tell visual flash is gated by a 200 ms cooldown that yields a design-ceiling cadence of ≤ 5 fires/sec — the IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT photosensitivity guard at the worst-case SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 buffer-flush chain cadence.

**Cooldown gate**: a SETTLED→SLIPPING transition's `LeadingFaceFlash` is suppressed (parameter stays at 0.0) iff:

```
t_transition − t_prior_fade_zero < 200 ms
```

where `t_prior_fade_zero` is the wall-clock instant at which the previous tell's `LeadingFaceFlash` parameter reached 0.0 at the end of its decay tail (NOT the previous transition's onset).

**Per-flash visible duration** (composition of §3 Commitment-Tell parameters):

```
flash_visible_duration_s = 2_frame_hold + 50 ms decay
                         = max(33 ms @ 60 fps, 66 ms @ 30 fps) + 50 ms
                         = 83 ms (60 fps) | 116 ms (30 fps)
```

**Effective cadence under sustained buffer-flush chains** (the worst case):

```
fire_cycle_s = flash_visible_duration_s + cooldown_window_s
             = 83 ms + 200 ms = 283 ms  (at 60 fps)
effective_cadence_hz = 1 / 0.283 ≈ 3.53 fires/sec  (at 60 fps)
```

The 200 ms cooldown is dimensioned so the effective cadence lands ≈ 3.5 fires/sec at 60 fps under sustained chains — comfortably below the **design ceiling of ≤ 5 fires/sec** that AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE (§8) enforces. The design ceiling is the suppression contract; the ≈ 3.5 fires/sec figure is the expected steady-state outcome.

**Theoretical-maximum transition cadence at the SLIP_TWEEN safe-floor** (for context only — this is the transition rate the cooldown is sized against, NOT the flash rate):

```
transitions_per_sec_at_floor = 1 / SLIP_TWEEN_safe_floor = 1 / 0.10 = ~10 transitions/sec
flash_suppression_ratio = 1 − (effective_cadence / transitions_per_sec)
                        = 1 − (3.53 / 10) ≈ 65 % of transitions suppressed under sustained chains
```

The 65 % suppression ratio is consistent with AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE Setup A's "at least 50 % of buffer-flush transitions in the sample window" suppression assertion (§8).

**PEAT compliance sketch (in-development sanity-check only — NOT the formal certification gate)**: at the worst-case observed cadence ≈ 3.5 fires/sec, peak luminance ±0.80 against ~0.20 ambient torso reflectance yields a relative contrast step of ~0.60 (60 %). PEAT's general-flash criterion permits higher cadences at reduced contrast than the 3 Hz / 100 %-contrast ceiling; the working-sketch product `flash_rate × contrast = 3.5 × 0.60 = 2.1` flashes·contrast/sec sits well below the 6.0 flashes·contrast/sec quick-look heuristic. **Formal PEAT certification is the Harding Flash and Pattern Analyzer (FPA) tool's PASS verdict on the Setup A recorded video, NOT the working-sketch product** — see AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE PEAT compliance assertion.

### F-HARDCUT-RAMP — Triple-overlap slip-cue gain ramp envelope (R11a §4 C.3 = R11a-15; B-AUDIO-2 author decision pending)

When the triple-overlap resolution table (§3 Audio) requires the active slip cue to be HARD-CUT under simultaneous buffer-drop + near-miss conditions, the gain ramp shape and duration determine whether the cut reads as a clean priority resolution or as a click artefact.

**Locked parameters (R11a §4 C.3)**:
- **Ramp duration**: ≤ 5 ms total, applied at suppression onset (the moment buffer-drop dispatches into an active slip cue with a concurrent active near-miss).
- **Direction**: slip cue gain ramps from authored level (or current ducked level if mid-near-miss-duck) to silence.
- **Constraint**: the ramp must end before the buffer-drop click's spectral peak (a 300–600 Hz dry percussive < 80 ms total) reaches its first zero-crossing — otherwise the slip residue muddies the buffer-drop attack.

**Ramp shape**: [R12a-PENDING — DR-PRES-RAMP, B-AUDIO-2]. The R11a triple-overlap resolution table specifies "≤ 5 ms ramp" but does not specify the gain curve. Two candidate shapes are under consideration for R12a:

- **(a) Linear gain ramp**: `g(t) = 1.0 − (t / 5 ms)` for `t ∈ [0, 5 ms]`. Simplest to implement (single-instruction per audio sample); has a discontinuity in the gain derivative at `t = 0` and `t = 5 ms` which can produce a faint click artefact at sample-rate-aligned ramp endpoints. Cheaper at MVP scale.
- **(b) Raised-cosine gain ramp**: `g(t) = 0.5 × (1 + cos(π × t / 5 ms))` for `t ∈ [0, 5 ms]`. Continuous gain derivative at both endpoints (slope = 0 at `t = 0` and `t = 5 ms`); no click artefact at endpoints; standard audio-engineering choice for short fade-outs of pitched / tonal content. Marginally more expensive per sample but trivial at single-cue scope.

**Author decision required (DR-PRES-RAMP — closes B-AUDIO-2 in R12a)**: select (a) or (b) and append the chosen formula to §3 Audio > Triple-overlap resolution table. Audio-director domain authority. Pre-R12a placeholder for implementation: use raised-cosine (b) as the safer default until DR-PRES-RAMP is decided — easier to relax later than to retrofit if linear introduces click artefacts on hardware.

**EC-16 — Triple-overlap audio: buffer-drop + slip + near-miss simultaneously active (R11a §4 C.3 = R11a-15 NEW).**
A player taps a 3rd slip while the buffer is full (mechanics §3 Rule 3 fires `EHapticEvent::BufferDrop` + buffer-drop audio sting) at the exact same frame that a slip cue from a prior accepted slip is still playing (`audio_cue_duration_s ≈ 93–168 ms`) AND Pull-Wave fires `TriggerNearMissBeat()` (near-miss swell 200–300 ms) for an unrelated near-miss event. All three audio cues are simultaneously eligible to play. Per the **triple-overlap resolution table** in §3 Audio: buffer-drop plays at full volume; the slip cue is HARD-CUT with a ≤ 5 ms ramp (buffer-drop priority hierarchy dominates the slip-vs-near-miss ducking carve-out — resolving toward HARD-CUT eliminates the contradictory "ducked-AND-suppressed" state on the slip cue); the near-miss swell plays at full volume (it is never ducked under any PM cue). Net audible result: buffer-drop click + near-miss swell, no slip cue. The slip cue's `commitment_tell_fire_count` increment for the underlying SETTLED→SLIPPING transition (if applicable) is unaffected — the counter reflects commitment, not audio render (parity with R11a-11 commitment-tell cadence cap which similarly decouples counter from visual flash; see `player-movement-mechanics.md` §8 AC-33 + §8 AC-25 + AC-COUNTER-* family). **EC-17 — AC-AUDIO-CUE-DUCKING reverse trigger order: slip dispatched during active near-miss swell (R11a §4 C.2 vacuous-case extension; B-AUDIO-1 author decision pending).**

**EC-19 — Haptic dispatch during OS Focus / Do-Not-Disturb / system-haptics-disabled state (R12a B-CERT-2 author decision pending; App Store cert risk).**
[R12a-PENDING — DR-PRES-HAPTIC-GATE closes B-CERT-2] A player has iOS Focus mode active (Work / Sleep / Driving / Do Not Disturb) OR Android Do Not Disturb engaged OR system-haptics disabled at the OS level. **EC-20 — Cadence-cap suppression at watchdog-degraded framerate (R11a §5 DR-D.3 — frame-rate-dependent flash duration).**
A device has degraded to sustained 30 fps (watchdog has broadcast `is_hw_performance_degraded == true`; platform `OnHardwarePerformanceBreach` fired). The §3 Commitment-Tell 2-frame hold at 30 fps is 66 ms (vs 33 ms at 60 fps). The 200 ms cadence-cap cooldown is wall-clock relative (NOT frame-relative); the cooldown gate timestamp comparison uses `t_prior_fade_zero` per §4 F-COMMIT-CADENCE-CAP. At 30 fps the flash visible duration is 116 ms (66 ms hold + 50 ms decay) vs 83 ms at 60 fps; the fire-cycle becomes `116 ms + 200 ms = 316 ms ≈ 3.16 fires/sec` effective cadence under sustained buffer-flush chains — still well below the 5 fires/sec design ceiling. No additional suppression logic needed at 30 fps. AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE Setup A is valid at both 60 fps and 30 fps; the suppression ratio shifts (≤ 50 % suppression at 60 fps; ≤ 37 % at 30 fps because fewer transitions occur per second at the slower framerate) but the design-ceiling assertion holds.

**EC-21 — Near-miss haptic dispatch during pause / resume-grace window (R11a §5 DR-D.4 + RSM Rule 19).**
The player has paused mid-run (`RSM.is_paused == true`); a near-miss event already in flight (Pull-Wave Rule 11 fired `TriggerNearMissBeat()` immediately before the pause). The opt-in haptic dispatch path runs through `TriggerNearMissBeat()` which executes synchronously when called. If the call site executes before RSM transitions to PAUSED, the haptic fires normally. If the call site executes after PAUSED (e.g., a frame-skip races RSM's pause transition), `TriggerNearMissBeat()` checks `RSM.is_paused` and short-circuits — no haptic dispatch, no visual Y-dip, no audio swell. On resume (RSM exits PAUSED into RUNNING during the 0.5 s `RESUME_GRACE_S` window per `run-state-machine.md` Rule 19): pending near-miss triggers from the pre-pause frame are NOT replayed — the player's resume input is the next dispatch path. No queued-haptic state survives pause.

**EC-22 — Audio cue dispatch during AudioDeviceManager interruption (iOS phone call / Android system audio takeover).**
The OS interrupts SLIPSTORM's audio session (incoming call / alarm / voice assistant). The UE AudioDeviceManager suspends playback. Slip / edge-absorb / near-miss / buffer-drop cues dispatched during the interruption window are silently dropped at the OS audio layer (UE AudioDeviceManager handles the suspend; no PM-side change). The visual signals (commitment-tell flash, slip-arc body lean, near-miss Y-dip, banner) continue to render normally — the visual / audio ownership split (§3 Audio-Visual Ownership Split) means visual signals are not contingent on audio dispatch. On audio-session resume, PM does NOT re-play dropped cues — the player's next slip/near-miss event begins fresh. The opt-in near-miss haptic IS still gated by `IHapticDispatch::IsSystemHapticsEnabled()` per EC-19; on iOS this typically returns `false` during an active phone call, so haptic is suppressed alongside audio.

### Commitment-Tell Rendering

**AC-29 (R11a §5 DR-D.3 amended — peak ±1.0 → ±0.80; pre-R11a ±1.0 assertion RETIRED)** *(visual AC — verified by playtest/screenshot/recording evidence):* Given PM is SETTLED and receives a valid slip input (RSM gating passes, SETTLED→SLIPPING transition occurs) AND ≥ 200 ms has elapsed since the prior commitment-tell's fade-to-zero (cadence-cap not engaged), then on the transition frame the `LeadingFaceFlash` material parameter is set to **±0.80** (leading face at 80 % white; the pre-R11a ±1.0 = 100 % peak is RETIRED — see R11a-11 + § Commitment-Tell). The parameter holds at ±0.80 for a minimum of 2 frames (≥ 33 ms at 60 fps; ≥ 66 ms at 30 fps), then decays to 0.0 over ≤ 50 ms. Total visible duration ≥ 83 ms at 60 fps. Does not fire for edge-absorb inputs (PM remains SETTLED; no commitment was made). `commitment_tell_fire_count` increments by 1 on the transition frame regardless of cadence-cap suppression of the visual flash (the counter reflects commitment, not flash render — see AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE for the suppression case; mechanics §8 AC-33 verifies the counter increment path).

**AC-COMMIT-FLASH-CADENCE (R11a §5 DR-D.3 NEW — closes IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT photosensitivity defect at SLIP_TWEEN=0.10 buffer-flush chains; classification = Integration/Visual — automated dispatch test + manual evidence with screen recording; ADVISORY gate; Polish-phase BLOCKING)**: Verifies the joint effect of the ±0.80 luminance peak (AC-29) AND the ≤ 5 fires-per-second cadence cap keeps the commitment-tell within IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT (photosensitive epilepsy) thresholds at the worst-case observed cadence.

**Setup A (cadence-cap engaged — suppression case):** Configure `SLIP_TWEEN_DURATION_S = 0.10s` (safe-range floor) + `audio_cue_ratio = 0.93` (R11a §4 DR-C.1 = R11a-13 default; raised from R10a's 0.85 because 0.85 is now below the new safe-range floor 0.89). Drive PM through a sustained buffer-flush chain: SETTLED at Center, queue `slip-right`, on tween completion queue another `slip-left` (chains via buffer-flush at each tween completion). Maintain the chain for at least 2.0 s wall-clock (≥ 20 SETTLED→SLIPPING transitions at SLIP_TWEEN=0.10). Note: `audio_cue_ratio` does not affect the visual-flash cadence cap (the 200 ms cooldown is gated on `LeadingFaceFlash`-fade-to-zero, independent of the audio cue duration); the ratio is captured here only for setup completeness.

**Asserted under Setup A**:
- `commitment_tell_fire_count` increments by exactly 1 per SETTLED→SLIPPING transition (no skipped increments — the counter reflects commitment, not flash render).
- The **observed peak `LeadingFaceFlash` cadence** measured at the material parameter dispatcher is ≤ 5 fires/sec across the 2.0 s sample window (= ≤ 10 visible flashes over the window). Visible flashes are counted by sampling the `LeadingFaceFlash` parameter every frame and counting rising edges from 0.0 → ±0.80; a rising edge that occurs within 200 ms of the prior fade-to-zero is suppressed (parameter stays at 0.0) and NOT counted.
- Suppressed-transition assertion: for at least 50 % of the buffer-flush transitions in the sample window, `LeadingFaceFlash` remains at 0.0 across the transition frame and the subsequent 2 frames (suppression confirms the 200 ms gate engaged) — `commitment_tell_fire_count` still increments for these transitions.

**Setup B (cadence-cap NOT engaged — normal flash case, sanity check):** Configure `SLIP_TWEEN_DURATION_S = 0.15s` (safe-range default). Drive PM through 5 isolated slips (each waits ≥ 500 ms after the prior tell's fade-to-zero before the next input — well outside the 200 ms cooldown).

**Asserted under Setup B**:
- All 5 commitment-tells render at ±0.80 peak per AC-29 (no suppression).
- Cadence ≤ 2 fires/sec across the sample window (far below the 5/sec cap; verifies the cap does NOT suppress normal-cadence flashes).

**PEAT compliance assertion (BINDING — gates Polish-phase certification readiness; formula below is a WORKING SKETCH, not the formal criterion)**: at the worst-case observed cadence under Setup A (≤ 5 fires/sec design ceiling, ~3.5 fires/sec effective — see effective-cadence math in AC-29's 200 ms cooldown vs ~83 ms flash duration; 80 % peak luminance against the avatar's dark torso material — typical reflectance ≈ 0.2 in the base palette), the resulting flash sequence is intended to satisfy IEC 61966-2-2 PEAT thresholds. **Working sketch for in-development sanity-check**: the 80 % peak against ~20 % ambient gives a relative luminance step of ~0.60 (60 % contrast); under PEAT's general red-flash + general-flash criteria, reduced contrast permits higher cadences than the 3 Hz / 100 % contrast ceiling. For *in-development sanity*, the test apparatus may compute the flash rate × contrast product (`≤ 6 flashes × contrast-units per second`, where contrast-units = (peak − ambient) / ambient on a normalized 0–1 luminance scale) as a quick-look heuristic — this is NOT the formal PEAT criterion. **Formal certification (Polish-phase BLOCKING)**: real PEAT testing is run via the actual Harding Flash and Pattern Analyzer (FPA) tool OR the W3C-PEAT app on the recorded Setup A video. The certification gate is the Harding/PEAT tool's PASS verdict, NOT the working-sketch product.
